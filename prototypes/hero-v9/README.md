## The floor, re-created from igloo.inc's snow
Sam asked for the floor to look exactly like igloo's. Three Blender variants were built from the hero camera and judged against four igloo screenshots by three reviews (surface, shape, tone): variant a won on composition (flank knolls, open centre, foreground mounds), with b's tone and stone scatter and c's measurement harness grafted on. The integrated floor (`blender/floor_final.py`, post curve `blender/floor_final_post.py`, LUT `blender/floor_lut.json`, checks `blender/floor_judge.py` and `blender/verify_bake.py`) adds:
- a pale blue-grey snow (near ground sRGB about 0.42/0.44/0.49 after the page curve, B/R 1.15 to 1.18, dark tenth B/R 1.27), neutral shadows, no violet;
- wind-sculpted drifts and soft mounds, a central plain under the logo held 0.30 below the sightline, a logo pad at y -3.6;
- 70 small dark stones so a dozen show in frame, albedo lifted so none crush to black;
- a soft contact shadow baked under the floating logo (a shadow-only proxy of the mark, invisible to the camera);
- depth haze toward the far valley.
The 4096 bake carries the macro lighting only; the page's own crumb grain sits on top, because at 18 to 35 texels per unit a bake cannot hold igloo's 0.03-unit grain.

**Page side.** The bake came through a stop too bright on the lit faces while the shaded flanks matched, so the ground shader applies a soft power curve in linear light (`gExp` 0.45, `gGamma` 0.7, fitted band by band against the igloo hero shot: lit bands now within 3 percent) and a gentle near-camera falloff (`gNear` 0.8, full by 20 units), which also mirrors igloo's darker foreground and keeps the headline at 4.8:1 on desktop and 4.6:1 on the phone (the phone sub-line goes to Source Serif 4 600 so it stays inside AA as bold text). Fog brightness raised to 0.4 so the wisps read on pale snow and the far ridge melts into the night instead of cutting out. New Tune sliders: Ground exposure, Ground contrast, Near ground.

Known gaps against igloo, being fixed in a further bake round: the bake's streak layer reads as brushed smear at 2x (igloo is soft isotropic mottling), a flat band where the sightline cap meets the plain, little relief in the mid field (igloo has stacked drifts with crescent scarps), the far ridge as one flat plane, the left mound's crest. igloo's camera also looks down onto its terrain; ours sits 1.1 units above the floor pitched up, so its big hazy hills behind the subject cannot read the same way here. A higher-camera framing was rendered for comparison (`blender/` notes) and is a decision for Sam.

## v9.1: countdown and scroll cue (same day)
Sam asked for the hero copy to go: no headline, no sub-line, no button, plain and simple, with a countdown in its place and a scroll cue so people know to scroll.
- **Countdown** to launch, bottom left where the copy was: an Open Sans 400 eyebrow "Launch in" and four Open Sans 600 numerals with tabular figures (days, hours, minutes, seconds), labels in Open Sans 400 under them, all Pearl Whisper so they hold AA on the pale snow (measured 5.9:1 numerals, 5.2:1 labels, 6.0:1 eyebrow at 1280). A plain inline script ticks it once a second, so it runs even if the 3D module never starts. The target is one constant: 1 November 2026, 00:00 IST, the date skreed.in hands over to skreed.com (Sam wrote 1 October; that date had already passed on the day of the request, so the documented launch date is used; change `T` in the countdown script if another date is meant).
- **Scroll cue**, bottom centre: the word "Scroll" over a thin 40 px line along which a short Pearl Whisper run slides down every 1.8 s with a real easing. Not an arrow. It fades as soon as the pull-back starts and holds still under reduced motion. On the phone the countdown sits higher so the cue has its own room.
- The "Reserve my shade" button and its scroll handler are removed.

## v9.7: snow floor round 6 (2026-10-09)
The last automatic floor round. Fixed against round 5, judged on the page render:
- **No holes:** the black half-disc and specks were stones whose undersides were buried in the bake's fine mesh, so they baked black, but the coarser page mesh left them exposed. Buried vertices now take their stone's exposed colour, stones are floored at luma 0.17, and the eight stones the page mesh exposes are dropped. On the page no floor pixel sits below luma 50.
- **Grain direction:** igloo's fine grain descends to the right (minus 10 to minus 26 degrees on screen); ours rose. The streak headings are now blended by world x so the screen heading is about minus 15 degrees (measured minus 12 to minus 15, coherence 0.78 to 0.85).
- **Right lee lifted:** the relief boost now acts on lit faces only, and a camera-side fill lights the swell's lee (mean luma 33 to 92).
- **Drifts:** four crescent fronts in front of the logo pad, a sloped skirt at the ridge base (no flat-topped wall), shallower pits the page mesh can carry, the page mesh low-passed so it agrees with the baked shading.
- **Far planes:** hazed toward their own band mean with distance; the bake's cooling step pushed them green on the page, so the shipped texture is a re-post of the saved raw bake without it (far-plane green over red 1.023, igloo 1.01 to 1.03). Re-posting takes 40 seconds instead of a 60-minute bake (`blender/floor_repost.py`).
- **Tone on the page,** igloo over ours: far left 0.99, far right 0.79, mid left 0.95, mid right 0.99, near centre 0.95, near right 1.07.
- **Still different from igloo** (stopping here; see `shot-floor-vs-igloo.png`): igloo's crust is finer and denser (2 to 4 px grain with small dark pits) where ours reads as broader smears; the left crest is a smooth dome where igloo's mound breaks into 30 to 60 px chunks; the mid-right relief is about 10 percent flatter than igloo's. igloo's camera also looks down onto its terrain while ours sits low and pitched up, which limits how much of the ground's form can show.

## v9.4: preloader, logo lighting (same day)
**Preloader, "Plot, scan, cut"** (igloo's intro re-read for Skreed; research: igloo stacks a decorative ASCII ticker bound to nothing over an 8 s WebGL intro in which a wireframe cage and an outline of the igloo pop in with counting numbers, the igloo prints top-down through its wire, then the camera descends). Ours: from first paint the page is real (logotype, countdown, night), and where the mark will stand a dimension-line scan head plots the ten block outlines as a technical drawing in step with the real load (three.js import, each block built, the sky and ground textures, the scene, each material compiled, two real frames), with construction ticks, part numbers 01 to 10 in the hero's label style, wire-box depth fading in behind each block, and a counter under the mark reading "nnn / 240" in Open Sans tabular figures that never runs ahead of the data (the last 24 only after the first real frame). Then the head returns and cuts the drawing into the live hero, which holds its pose until the cut (the projection matches the module's camera within 0.001 px at 1280 and 390) and ramps its parallax, shake, bob and breath in over 2 s. Honest states: "Slow connection" while the network milestones are pending, "Still loading" otherwise, "You are offline. The countdown still runs." offline, the drawing as a poster with the fallback copy if the module never starts (3 to 27 ms after a refused CDN) or hangs 20 s. Reduced motion: the finished drawing at once and a 0.4 s fade. Budget: 9,985 B raw, 4.8 KB gzipped, generated from pieces_10.json by build.py so loader and hero never drift. Verified under the strict CSP by two independent harnesses (134 of 139 checks, the five fails one defect since fixed). Hooks: `__skreedLoader.setProgress(p)`, `.cut()`, `.state()`, `.fail(text)`.

**Logo lighting.** Research (three lenses: cinematography of black on black, real-time look development including igloo's and ciao's rigs, and a per-face luminance analysis of our render) agreed that with faces at 1 to 3 percent albedo no key can lift a black face; separation comes from edges and reflections. The shader now carries a physically based rig: a large soft key front-left, a hard kicker behind-left (where the sky is blackest) and a counter kicker behind-right, the snow floor and the night sky as an analytic environment, a 3 px bevel ring on every block edge (normal-only, no vertex moves) that catches the kickers, edge wear, cut-face occlusion and a knee so nothing on the black blooms at rest. Three presets were rendered and judged by three lenses (DP, retoucher with numbers, brand): "Cinematic rim" won 8/10 from all three; the shipped default is that preset with the judges' tweaks (albedo 0.017 so the key-lit face stays under sRGB 40, kicker 2.4, counter kicker 0.75 and lifted to y 7, floor 0.24, occlusion 0.3). Measured: faces sRGB 25 to 37 (matte black), bevel ring L* 27 to 45, silhouette median contrast 17 L* on desktop and 15 on the phone against 8.5 before; no halo, no colour cast. Tune section "Logo light" exposes every value.

## Logotype glitch, in our shades
The classic glitch splits red and blue. Ours splits a near-complementary pair from the hero's ten family shades (OKLCH hues 130 to 170 degrees apart, the same theory behind a chromatic split): Sky and Crimson, Amethyst and Sunbeam, Lawn and Rouge, Sky and Mango. Each burst picks one pair; about 40 percent of the slabs get two ghost copies of the wordmark pulled 1.5 to 2.7 percent of the width to either side, opacity balanced by lightness so Sunbeam and Amethyst read with the same weight, with the Pearl Whisper copy on top so the colour shows only as fringes. The intro burst uses Sky and Crimson, the pair the mark itself shows at rest. Tune: "Colour split" toggle. Off under reduced motion with the rest of the glitch.

## Logo lighting (sliders in, defaults off)
The matte black mark on the black sky needs cinematic separation. The shader now has a rim kicker (direction by azimuth and elevation), a GGX specular with roughness, a floor bounce from the snow and a violet sky ambient, all at 0 by default pending the lighting research and a judged preset.
