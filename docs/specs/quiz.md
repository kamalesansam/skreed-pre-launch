## 4. Motion plan

**Libraries.** None new, no GSAP. The hand-off is the hero island's second wipe (the v11 composite) with its far side changed to the fog ground. The DOM motion is section 2's decode (`src/scripts/quiz/decode.ts`, D31): each character's opacity, and while it rolls its glyph, written per animation frame (`requestAnimationFrame`) through the CSSOM, plus one Web Animations fade on the swatches' opacity. Since 2026-10-20 the answer pictures (`src/scripts/quiz/picture.ts`, D38) take the same decode on twelve strips of a photograph (below). No transform on the text, no `will-change`, and no CSS transition anywhere in the quiz but the autofill rules' 86400 s delay, which animates nothing (D35): what changes in the text is each glyph's opacity and, while it rolls, its character, swapped for another Plex Mono glyph of the same width (every advance is 600 units), so no line changes width; a picture's strips change their opacity and, while they roll, the slice they show, and its layer one scale. CHECKLIST C3's "transform and opacity only" holds for everything in the text that moves, and the character swap is part of E-S3-motion (CLAUDE.md), as section 2's labels swap theirs. The pictures' reveal was built on 2026-10-20 at Sam's request ("have some sort of animation when the text is appearing dont just straight up blankly slap the image on the screen on hover"), but his approval of E-S3-motion on 2026-10-19 covered the text's decode only, so it waits for his confirmation as part of that exception (section 10, 17): each strip's slice, the counterpart of the character swap, is a `background-position` written per frame, outside C3's "transform and opacity only", and the layer's scale is a transform, inside C3 but outside the exception's "no transform" as he approved it. The text is driven by clicks and keys, the pictures by a pointer entering and leaving an answer, keyboard focus and a finger's press: nothing follows a pointer and nothing reveals on scroll. Reduced motion comes from `src/scripts/motion.ts` (`reducedMQ`, read live at every frame of the decode). The constants in `deck.ts` are `DUR_MARK` 120 ms (the hold on the answer taken, a delay, not an animation), `DUR_SWATCH` 400 ms, and `GUARD` 350 ms with `GUARD_REDUCED` 150 ms (an input guard, not an animation: a press on an answer or Back within that time of its question being put on screen is dropped; D36); `EASE_STANDARD`, `EASE_IN`, `DUR_OUT`, `DUR_CARD` and `DUR_REDUCED` went with the fly-off, so the quiz no longer uses DESIGN.md's `--dur-out` and `--dur-card`.

**The decode, section 2's label animation constant for constant.** `decode.ts` takes its numbers and its roll from `src/scripts/hero/section2/labels.ts` (measured on igloo), and `tests/unit/quiz-decode.test.ts` reads both files and fails if they part:
- Reveal: a left-to-right alpha wipe over 0.4 s (`REVEAL_ALPHA`). Glyph i of n, at x = i / (n - 1), takes opacity `clamp(11u - 10x)` with u = t / 0.4, so the first character is fully in at 0.036 s and the last at 0.4 s. Meanwhile each glyph rolls through the five glyphs after it in its block of eight in the character table, counting down to itself: `roll(ch, k)` clears the code point's last three bits for the block and shows the block plus (the offset plus k) modulo 8, with k = `floor((1 - clamp(2v - x)) * 5.753) % 8` and v = t / 0.75 (`ROLL_STEPS` 5.753), so the first character shows its own glyph from about 0.31 s and the last from about 0.69 s; the element is flagged settled at 0.75 s (`REVEAL_ROLL`).
- Hide: a right-to-left alpha wipe over 0.2 s (`HIDE`): opacity `clamp(11(1 - u) - 10x)` with u = t / 0.2, never above where the glyph stood when the wipe began; no roll.
- Reduced motion: a 0.2 s fade (`REDUCED`), in or out, every glyph of the element together, no roll.
- Only printable ASCII rolls (U+0021 to U+007E). Plex Mono has no U+201B or U+201F, which the roll of a curly quote would reach, so the curly quotes, the no-break space, the ellipsis and anything else outside ASCII fade in place without rolling, and no glyph ever falls back to another font mid-roll, which would change the line's width (D33). Every glyph a rolling character passes through is in the two Plex Mono files (the unit test). Spaces are spans too, so an underlined control shows no piece of its underline before its words.
- A screen's elements start one after another (`showAll`), 0.05 s apart, the step shrinking so the last starts within 0.6 s: question 1 (the count, the question, seven answers and, since 2026-10-26, Skip quiz: ten elements) starts its last at 0.45 s and is settled at 1.2 s; questions 2 to 10 (with Back and Skip quiz, eleven elements) at 0.5 s and 1.25 s (nine and ten elements, 0.4 and 1.15 s and 0.45 and 1.2 s, before the quiz gate, `docs/specs/quiz-gate.md` 4); the result with the gift (22 or 23 elements; 16 or 17 without it) at 0.6 s and 1.35 s. A screen's elements wipe out together (`hideAll`).
- A start in the same direction while an element's run is under way joins that run (its promise waits on the same end) instead of restarting it, as labels.ts's `setOn()` does, so no glyph flashes back. `conceal()` puts an element straight to hidden with no animation, as `set()` puts one straight to shown.
- `#find` carries `data-decoding` while any text moves; the frames, the contrast script and the e2e wait for it to go.
- A hidden document draws no frames, so when the page is hidden, or becomes hidden mid-run, every running element goes straight to its end state; a background tab never stalls the quiz.

**The answer picture's reveal (`picture.ts`): the decode on a photograph.** Sam, 2026-10-20: "have some sort of animation when the text is appearing dont just straight up blankly slap the image on the screen on hover". The picture takes the text's own timing and roll, imported from `decode.ts` (`REVEAL_ALPHA`, `REVEAL_ROLL`, `ROLL_STEPS`, `HIDE`, `REDUCED`):
- The frame is cut into 12 vertical strips (`STRIPS`), each `calc(100% / 12 + 1px)` wide so that no seam shows between them. Each strip draws the picture as a background (the `img`'s `currentSrc`, sized and placed as `object-fit: cover` would, through the CSSOM), and the geometry is redone on resize and whenever the phone's frame is resized.
- Reveal: strip i, at x = i / 11, takes opacity `clamp(11u - 10x)` with u = t / 0.4, left to right, so the first strip is in at 0.036 s and the last at 0.4 s. Meanwhile strip i shows the slice of strip (i + k) mod 12, with k = `floor((1 - clamp(2v - x)) * 5.753) % 8` and v = t / 0.75, so each strip counts down through the slices up to five strips to its right (wrapping round) to its own, as a glyph rolls through the glyphs after it: the first strip holds its own slice from about 0.31 s and the last from about 0.69 s, and the picture is settled at 0.75 s. Over the same 0.75 s the picture's layer (`div.qz-shot`) scales from 1.03 to 1 on `transform`, eased out (1 - (1 - v)^3), inside the frame's `overflow: hidden`.
- Hide: a right-to-left wipe over 0.2 s: opacity `clamp(11(1 - u) - 10x)` with u = t / 0.2, never above where the strip stood when the wipe began, every strip on its own slice; no roll. The layer's scale goes from where it stood when the wipe began to 1 over the same 0.2 s, linearly, so a picture hidden mid-reveal (between 1 and 1.03) finishes its settle as it goes and no scale outlasts the wipe; once the reveal has settled it is 1 already. The transform is removed when the wipe ends.
- Switching answers: the outgoing picture wipes out while the incoming one decodes in over it (the layer coming in is drawn last). A show or a hide started while one in the same direction runs joins it. A new question takes every picture off at once, with no animation (`clear()`).
- Reduced motion: a 0.2 s fade, in or out, every strip together, no roll and no scale.
- A hidden document draws no frames, so a picture there goes straight to its end state, as the text does.

**Scoring (`scoreQuiz` in `src/config/quiz.ts`, pure, unit tested).** The ten answers are ten personality ids, card 1 first. The personality picked most often wins (Sam: "maximum answers corresponding to"). On a tie at the top, the tied personality the visitor picked most recently wins: the loop walks the answers from card 10 back to card 1 and returns the first whose personality has the top count (D24). Any ten answers give exactly one personality, so "no results" cannot happen; any other number of answers throws. Every personality is reachable (ten of its own answers give it). If every answer were equally likely, 93,710,442 of the 282,475,249 possible answer sets (7 to the power 10), 33.2 percent, would tie at the top, so the tie rule decides often. The worked cases of `tests/unit/quiz.test.ts`: four answers for The Spark against three for The Main Character, two for The Quiet Curator and one for The Dreamer give The Spark; main, curator, main, curator, main, curator, spark, optimist, rebel, dreamer ties The Main Character and The Quiet Curator at three, and The Quiet Curator was picked last of the two (card 6): The Quiet Curator; main, curator, spark, optimist, rebel, dreamer, strategist, main, curator, spark ties The Main Character, The Quiet Curator and The Spark at two, and The Spark was picked last (card 10): The Spark.

**The shade draw (`pickShade` in `src/config/quiz.ts`).** `finish()` calls `pickShade(p)` once per completed quiz: the index is the random number times the group's size, rounded down and capped at the last shade (`Math.min(p.shades.length - 1, Math.floor(rand() * p.shades.length))`, `rand` defaulting to `Math.random`), so each shade of a seven-shade group comes up one time in seven and each of The Spark's six one time in six; the recommended (first) shade is not favoured. The drawn shade takes the large swatch, the heading, the family line and the link; the others, in group order, are the compatible legend. "Not quite? Try again" and the next `finish()` draw again, so the same answers can give another shade of the same group (D2). The draw is neither stored nor seeded: a reload and the same answers draw afresh. `rand` is injectable for the unit test, which proves every shade of every group reachable and the others always the rest of the group in order.
