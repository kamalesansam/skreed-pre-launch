# Section 4: the entrance and the Skreed universe (spec)

Written 2026-10-16 from Sam's brief of the same day. Status: the plan for the build loop (`plan`, then `build`, then `review`); the numbers measured from the reference live in `docs/specs/evidence/section-4-measurements.md` and are quoted here with their source. Nothing of the reference ships: the hall is re-created by measurement in our own geometry, light and code, as sections 1 and 2 match igloo and the family pages match ciao.

**Sam's brief, verbatim (2026-10-16).** "let's go to the section 4, which is a grey room playable interactive space, the users get a FPP view inside a grey entrance, www.clicktorelease.com.zip check out this zip file which i placed inside the folder, and get the exact room, remove the metal ball in between, and add a door, once the user moves near the door, using arrow keys, or if they're on mobile, they just scroll to move to the entrance, the door i mean, and after that there's a button near the door that appears, which says enter skreed universe, like how the button appears in roblox for example, just as a reference, now once they click on the button, it will make an animation where you're moving horizonatally and enter a fully colorful room, the animation is like it makes the screen entirely bright and white, and then there's all colors moving in the screeen and finally enters the room, which has different heights of pedestals where diffenrent products from the skreed catalog is placed on the pedestal, and when hovered, text appears for that specific product".

**The reference.** The zip is a saved copy of "cru·ci·form" (Jaume Sanchez and Emil Karlsson, clicktorelease.com): a long grey concrete hall with two ceiling light strips, a row of floor-edge lights along each wall, a chrome orb halfway down, a music-driven camera, and a post stack (vignette, colour split, depth of field, bloom, grain). It is an untrusted upload: it was read in its own folder, nothing in it was run, and none of its files, textures, shaders, music or strings are used. What we take is measurements.

## 1. Goal

Turn the quiz's result into a walk: the visitor steps into Skreed's grey entrance, reaches the door, and enters a room of colour where the real products stand on pedestals and each one tells the visitor what it is. It serves brand goal 1 (the 240 shades and the products as the thing itself) and goal 2 (a reason to keep scrolling and to remember the site), and it hands the visitor to the reserve and share sections that follow.

## 2. Phone-first wireframe in words

### 390 x 844, 3D path

**The hand-off from section 3 (the third wipe).** Section 3 ends with the gift panel on the fog. The next element is `section#room`, a 1300svh track (1500svh once entered) with a sticky 100svh stage inside it, so the stage stays pinned through the loop screen and the one empty tail screen after it (section 5). Its progress `q` is in screens of its own height, read from its own rect: `q = -rect.top / (rect.height / N)` with `N` 13 (15 once entered), one `getBoundingClientRect` read per frame and never `innerHeight`, so the quiz's variable height above it does not matter and iOS's address bar (about 13 percent of the viewport as it collapses and expands mid-walk) cannot move the camera (B4). Over the first screen (`q` 0 to 1) the hero's composite wipes a third time, the same diagonal cut of ice shards as the two before it: on the near side the fog ground (section 3's background, nothing on it by now), on the far side the hall seen from its start station. The fog holds still under the cut as section 2 did (`uParA` 0). If the hall's assets are not in yet the fog stays the whole frame (`uProgress` 0): the first wipe waits by holding the hero's follower (`loop.ts` line 59), but `q` is the section's rect and native scroll, which nothing holds, so when the hall becomes ready while `#room` is in view the third wipe plays over 1 s on a clock (a cut under `still3d`'s `uCalm`) with the camera already at the `q` reached; meanwhile a square Pearl Whisper panel (`.qz-panel`, Urban Slate text, `--radius-none`) at the bottom of the stage reads "Loading the entrance..." (Open Sans 600, `--fs-label`, 44 px tall, inside the gutters).

**The hall (measured; sections 1 and 2 of the evidence file).** A straight concrete corridor 11.88 m long, 3.15 m wide and 3.99 m high (the reference's hall at the demo's own scale, which treats the 4 m bounding height as metres). Grey throughout: the walls, floor and ceiling share one dark blue-grey concrete tone and the only colour is light. The long walls carry floating raised panels, 0.17 m off the floor and stopping 0.09 m under the ceiling: a 7.6 m panel on the east part of each wall standing 0.11 m proud, a 2.8 m panel on the west part standing 0.05 m proud; each end wall carries a 3.0 m wide panel 0.12 m proud with a 0.07 m margin to the side walls, and a floor-level slot 0.16 m deep and 0.09 m high runs under each end wall. A transept slot 0.65 m wide cuts through both side walls 3.0 m from the west end, 0.51 m deep, full height, open at both ends to white (the brightest place in the hall). Five fixtures hang under the ceiling, rounded boxes 0.22 x 1.66 m dropping 0.055 m, at 5.88 and 3.53 m west of the middle, 0.07 m east of it, and 3.20 and 5.79 m east, in a cool white (about 7400 K), their undersides white on screen. Sixteen warm lights (about 4500 K) sit in the gap under the east panels, eight per wall at a 1.0 m pitch from 1.5 m west of the middle to 0.35 m short of the east panel, each throwing a small pool on the floor and a wedge up the base wall; none west of the transept, so the west end is the darkest part of the hall. The floor carries seams across it every 0.66 m and one along it, the panels horizontal seams at 1.13, 2.23 and 3.23 m above the floor, all baked a third darker; the concrete's grain is sub-millimetre and reads as a sparkle on the glossy floor. The reference's orb is not built (Sam). The west end wall carries the door, cut through its panel and base wall on the centre line: two leaves together 1.6 m wide and 2.6 m tall, set 0.18 m into a reveal that interrupts the floor slot, in Urban Slate (a render parameter, as the hero's light colours are), with a 6 mm seam of white light between the leaves that brightens as the visitor nears. The floor reflects about a fifth of the hall face-on and two fifths at a grazing angle (the reference's specular), so it carries the fixtures and the pools; our own cubemap, rendered once from the hall's centre and box-projected, is the reflection. Light bakes into our own lightmap (section 6); the seams and the grain are procedural in the shader; nothing is a texture from the reference.

**The start station (`q` = 1).** The camera stands 0.6 m in from the east end wall at 0.9 m above the floor (the reference's one eye-level shot, 0.908 units, section 3 of the evidence file), on the centre line, looking level down the corridor at the door 11.2 m away; vertical field of view 60 degrees on laptops, and on phones the hero's own portrait rule (`stage/resize.ts`: zoom `min(1, 1.25 x aspect)`, which widens the vertical field to 90 degrees at 390 x 844) so the horizontal field stays near 50 degrees on every phone instead of the reference's 39. Nothing moves on its own: the fixtures are steady, the seam of light is steady, there is no music. At the bottom of the stage, inside the gutters and above the safe area, one square Pearl Whisper panel (`.qz-panel`, Urban Slate text) in Open Sans 600 `--fs-label`: "Scroll to walk to the door." (on devices with a keyboard: "Scroll or use the arrow keys to walk to the door."). It stays until the visitor has walked 1 m, then goes (200 ms, `--ease-standard`) and does not return.

**The walk (`q` 1 to 9).** Scrolling is walking: eight screens carry the camera 10.05 m west, from 0.6 m inside the east wall to 1.2 m short of the west wall's door, past the transept crossing 3.3 m from the door (its open ends show white either side for a moment), through the hero's two scroll followers (0.075 then 0.15) so the step is smooth: 1.26 m per screen. The evidence file (section 3.6) proposed 0.5 m per screen so that a steady drag of 0.25 to 0.8 screens per second stays inside the reference's dolly bracket of 0.12 to 0.40 m per second; at 1.26 m per screen the same drag moves the camera at 0.32 to 1.0 m per second, above the bracket for most of its range, and a one-screen flick peaks well above the reference's fastest dolly (0.40). The gain stays at 1.26 so the walk is eight screens, pending Sam (section 11, item 8), and the followers' output is clamped at 1.0 m per second. On a phone the thumb does it; the pace is the visitor's. Nothing else moves: no head bob, no sway on touch. Page scrolling is never blocked (`touch-action` default; the stage is sticky, the track is plain document height).

**The door station (`q` 9 to 10).** The camera holds 1.2 m from the door. From 2.2 m out (`q` 8.2) the prompt appears, the way a Roblox proximity prompt does when the player comes within its activation distance (the Roblox default is 10 studs, about 2.8 m at Roblox's 0.28 m stud; 2.2 m keeps it inside the last two screens of the walk; evidence file, section 5): a primary button that fades in over 200 ms (opacity 0 to 1, `--ease-standard`, no scaling: Roblox's own look-alike sample fades in 0.2 s and never scales on appear; this fade is listed under E-S4-motion, section 10), anchored to the door's handle point projected into the screen each frame (the door's centre, 1.1 m up, 0.35 m right of the seam, projected from the camera with the pointer sway removed so the button never chases the pointer), clamped to sit inside the gutters and above the safe area; on a phone that puts it just below the door's centre. The button is in the DOM and in the Tab order for the whole section, never `hidden`: beyond 2.2 m it is visually hidden (the clip pattern, still in the accessibility tree), a keyboard focus on it while off-station snaps the page to the station (`scrollTo` the top of `q` 9.5, `behavior: 'instant'`, the section's followers snapped, section 2's `snapTo` pattern) so the ring appears where the button is visible, and a press while off-station (Enter, Space or an assistive tap) snaps to the station in the same task and then enters. It is the section's first tabbable element. It reads "Enter Skreed universe" (Ember Luxe, Urban Slate text, Open Sans 600 16 px, 48 px tall, square, DESIGN.md 4); on devices with a keyboard a 28 px square key hint reading "E" (Open Sans 600 at `--fs-label`, Urban Slate on Pearl Whisper, a 1 px Urban Slate hairline) sits at its left end, the Roblox key-chip shape; on touch there is no chip and the button itself is the tap target (Roblox: phones always tap the prompt). The two-tone focus ring. It takes focus when it appears unless an input has focus, so Enter works at once; E works only while the stage or the prompt has focus (WCAG 2.1.4, a letter shortcut active on focus only). Tap, click, Enter, Space or E all enter. Scrolling past the station before entering carries the visitor into the loop screen (the fourth wipe back to the hero, section 2); scrolling back brings the station and the prompt back.

**The transition (2.4 s, section 4 table).** On entering, the leaves swing inward 95 degrees over 0.9 s (ease-out) and the seam's light floods the frame; from 0.3 s the camera glides forward 1.6 m through the doorway over 0.9 s while the frame's luminance lifts to pure white by 1.0 s (a mix toward white in the transition pass, `section4/transition.ts`, one full-screen triangle drawn after the composite as D7 says; not a DOM overlay and not an edit to the composite's shader); from 1.0 to 1.9 s ten soft diagonal bands in the ten families' key shades (section 3, the same ten the gems use) sweep across the white from bottom left to top right, each band 0.12 screens wide with a 0.04 feather, ordered by relative luminance from the brightest to the darkest (Snow, Sunbeam, Mango, Sky, Silver, Rouge, Lawn, Cinnamon, Amethyst, Crimson) so the pass is one descent from the white into the room and no pixel sees more than one opposing luminance change (WCAG 2.3.1); the ribbon is 1.2 screens long and must travel 2.2 screens along the diagonal to enter and leave, so it moves 2.2 screens in 0.9 s (about 2.4 screens per second); from 1.6 s the universe fades in under the bands and the last band has left the frame by 1.9 s; at 2.4 s the camera stands in the room's doorway. The bands are a canvas effect of the transition pass and need exception E-S4-sweep (section 10). A text button "Skip" (the `.txtbtn` of DESIGN.md, Open Sans 600, Urban Slate) sits on a square Pearl Whisper panel 44 px tall (9.9:1 on any frame), bottom centre inside the gutters and above the safe area, from 0 s of the transition, and takes focus at the press (the prompt goes `hidden` at that moment, so focus must move); it, Escape, Enter, Space or a tap jump to 2.4 s, and at 2.4 s focus moves to the active product's button. Page scrolling is never blocked, but the camera ignores `q` during the transition, and at 2.4 s (or at the skip) the page is put at the top of `q` 10 (`scrollTo`, `behavior: 'instant'`, the section's followers snapped; nothing visible changes because the stage is pinned), so the universe's map starts where section 2 says.

**The look on screen (measured; evidence file sections 2 and 4).** The hall is rendered unlit, albedo times our lightmap, with no tone mapping and into 8-bit targets, because the reference's look comes from a post chain that clips: one output pass of the hall's own (`RenderPass` plus `OutputPass` under `NoToneMapping`, the `makePlainComposer` pattern, which also encodes the frame to sRGB for the composite: three 0.165 encodes to sRGB only for the null target, so a plain render target would hand the composite linear values and the measured #596169 would not hold) applies a vignette that multiplies the centre by 2 and the corners by 0.85 (normalised by the longer side so a portrait phone keeps the laptop's corners rather than going black), a bleach-bypass contrast curve, a static grain of plus or minus 0.5 percent (the hero's composite has no grain at rest and its fragment is pinned byte for byte to the v11.4 fixture by `tests/unit/glsl-v11.test.ts`, so nothing is added to it; E-S4-grain), and a colour split of up to 8 px at the corners and none at the centre; on laptops the hero's bloom pass screens a blurred copy over the frame as the reference does (no threshold, radius 0.8 percent of the frame); depth of field is not built (section 8). On screen the mid-hall floor reads about #596169 at the centre falling to about #131516 in the corners, every fixture clips to white and the pools stay warm.

**The universe (`q` 10 to 12 after entering; the section gains 200svh at that moment, and the page is put at the top of `q` 10 at 2.4 s so the map holds whatever `q` the prompt was pressed at; after entering, `q` below 10 shows the universe at yaw -60 degrees, never the hall again on this lap).** A round room 11 m across and 4.18 m high (the hall's height, so the doorway's proportions carry over). Its wall is ten bays of 36 degrees, each in one family's key shade, matte, in catalog order clockwise from the doorway: Snow, Sky, Rouge, Amethyst, Sunbeam, Cinnamon, Mango, Silver, Lawn, Crimson (ids in section 3). In front of the visitor an arc of pedestals 3.2 m away spanning 60 degrees either side, Urban Slate cylinders 0.5 m across at six heights (0.55, 0.85, 0.7, 1.0, 0.6, 0.9 m), each carrying one real product at exhibition scale (1.5 x life size): six pedestals now, two more when their assets arrive (section 3). The camera stands in the doorway at 1.0 m, field of view as in the hall. Scrolling the two screens pans the view from the leftmost pedestal to the rightmost (yaw -60 to +60 degrees, through the followers); the pedestal nearest the centre of the screen is the active one. Its label shows at the bottom of the stage, inside the gutters: a Pearl Whisper panel with the product's name in Poppins 700 `--fs-clause` (a `<span>` inside the product's button, never a heading element: "240 shades" carries a digit, A3) and its line in Open Sans 400 `--fs-legal` (the text of section 3). A tap on another pedestal (pointerdown to pointerup under 8 px and under 300 ms on `.rm-stage`, the quiz's rule in `deck.ts`, then one raycast at that point; the hit volume is the pedestal, the product and a 44 x 44 px screen-space pad, because a 1.5 x case 3.2 m away is about 21 x 39 px at 390 wide and a pedestal about 66 px; a miss does nothing) makes it active by scrolling the page to that pedestal's `q` (section 2's `snapTo` pattern, `scrollTo` with `behavior: 'smooth'`, instant under reduced motion, the followers following), so the yaw stays a function of `q` (D1). **The loop (Sam, 2026-10-16: "the last section always scrolls to the first section next, as a loop, there's no end").** The page has no bottom. One more screen follows the last thing on it (the universe's pan, or the door station if the visitor scrolls on without entering): over that screen the composite wipes a fourth time, the same ice-shard cut, from the frame on screen (the universe, or the hall) to the hero's rest frame (scene A at scroll 0: the logo whole in its blocks, the countdown and cue in place). The moment the cut has crossed the screen the page jumps to scroll 0 in one step (`scrollTo({top: 0, behavior: 'instant'})`, the followers snapped to 0), which shows exactly the frame the wipe just arrived at, so nothing is seen to change; the next scroll runs the hero's own map again (the pull-back, the first wipe, the gems, the quiz, the hall), and so on without end. The intro and the loader never replay; the quiz keeps its answers (a second lap finds the result still showing, with "Not quite? Try again"); the hall starts at its start station again; the universe stays entered (its pan range is still there). The DOM riders (the countdown, the cue) are in their scroll-0 state at the jump because the jump puts them there. On the poster tier the loop is a 300 ms fade to the top: the last section's end fades through `--night` to the first screen and the page jumps to 0 under the fade. ## 4. Motion plan

No library: the island's own loop for the camera and the shaders, the Web Animations API for the DOM (the pill, the prompt, the label, the skip). Everything DOM moves by transform and opacity.

| Trigger | What moves | How |
|---|---|---|
| `q` 0 to 1 | the third wipe | the composite's existing cut, `uProgress` = `q`, `uParA` 0 |
| scroll (`q` 1 to 9) | the camera's x | 1.26 m per screen through the two followers; nothing else |
| arrow keys (keyboard present) | the page | the sticky stage is focusable (`tabindex="0"`, `aria-label` "The entrance. Use the arrow keys to walk to the door.") and takes focus on a pointerdown inside it; while it or the prompt has focus, Up/W and Down/S scroll at 0.5 m per second while held (`scrollBy` per frame, the browser's smooth scrolling off for these calls, a 0.3 s ease in and out) and Left and Right turn the head 25 degrees for as long as they are held, lerped back on release; Escape blurs the stage (WCAG 2.1.2, no trap). Without focus the keys do what they always do on a page, so a keyboard user tabbing past the section is never hijacked (WCAG 2.1.4: W, S and E are letters, active on focus only; the arrows are not letters but follow the same rule for one behaviour) |
| pointer (hover: hover) | the head | yaw 6.3 degrees, pitch 2.25, lerped at the hero's parallax rate (0.035); held while the pointer is over the prompt or a product |
| 1 m walked | the hint panel | opacity 1 to 0, 200 ms, `--ease-standard`, then `hidden` (E-S4-motion) |
| 2.2 m from the door | the prompt | opacity 0 to 1, 200 ms, `--ease-standard`, no scale (section 2; E-S4-motion); the reverse on leaving |

**Reduced motion** (`motion.ts`, the same source as the hero): on the poster tier the stills (section 2); on the 3D path four cuts for the walk, no head sway, no key turn easing (the turn is a cut too), the prompt appears without scaling, the door does not swing, the transition is a 300 ms fade to white and a 300 ms fade in with no sweep and no dolly, the pan is three cuts, products do not lift. The seam light still brightens (it is a state, not an animation). Nothing in the section moves on its own for more than the 2.4 s transition, so WCAG 2.2.2 needs no control.

## 9. Decisions taken

- D1. One mechanism moves the visitor: scroll. The arrow keys scroll the page, so the walk has one source of truth, and a phone and a laptop are the same section (cheaper on a phone than a second input model).
- D2. The section's progress comes from its own rect, not from `scrollY / innerHeight`, because the quiz above it changes height with its states.
- D3. The hall is our own Blender model with a baked lightmap, not real-time lights: one unlit material, one draw call, and the bake can be as rich as the reference's baked look; the fixtures are emissive meshes, the floor's gloss a one-off cubemap. The grain is procedural so the lightmap stays smooth and small.
- D4. The orb is removed (Sam) and nothing replaces it; the hall's middle is empty so the door reads from the start.
- D5. The door is two leaves with a seam of light rather than a single slab: the seam gives the approach a cue and the swing gives the transition its first beat.
- D6. The prompt is a real primary button with a key hint, not a canvas drawing: it is the section's one CTA, it needs focus and a label, and the Roblox pattern it follows is "a UI element that appears at a distance" (the evidence file), not a drawing.
- D7. The transition's white-out and sweep are one full-screen triangle drawn last in the composite's own pass, from `uv` and a progress uniform (the GL Transitions "fadecolor" and "colorphase" forms, evidence file section 5), not a DOM overlay and not a second render-target pass: it has to blend two rendered frames the composite already holds, a DOM white over the canvas would double the fill on a phone, and a luminance-driven white-out would need an extra target the budget does not have.
- D8. The universe is round with ten bays, one per family, in the gems' key shades: it answers "fully colourful" with the product's own colours and ties to section 2, instead of inventing a palette (which rules 1, 21 and 24 forbid anyway).
- D9. Products at 1.5 x life size on an arc 3.2 m away: at life size a 20 cm case reads as a thumbnail at that distance on a phone; at 1.5 x it fills about a fifth of the screen height when active. The arc, not a grid, so the pan passes every pedestal once.
- D10. Six pedestals now, two wired for later: the products report shows no honest way to show the accessories today. The stand-ins for Snap and Armor are shown with a one-line note rather than left out, because the catalog sells them and Sam promised their files on 2026-10-14; Sam can switch them off (section 11).
- D11. Labels on phones are a bottom panel, on laptops a floating one: a floating label under a thumb is covered by the thumb.
- D12. The catalog's rule-17 words are dropped from the labels and the rest of each sentence kept, pending Sam.
- D13. The section does not hold the scroll at the door: a visitor who does not press can keep scrolling (the loop screen follows and the page returns to the hero); the station returns when they scroll back. Holding the page would break B3 and the back-and-forth of a phone.
- D14. The poster tier gets two stills and a list, not a video or a canvas: it is the tier that cannot draw.
