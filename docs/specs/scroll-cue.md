# The cue's words: "Scroll to see more"

Brief (2026-11-02): "Apart from the backgrounds, please also add some text like "Scroll to see more" wherever one has to scroll. It's not very clear otherwise that one has to scroll and keep scrolling, especially on the phone, and the arrows are not enough."

Grounded in six read-only maps of the code (the hero's track and section 2, the quiz and its gate, section 4, the family pages, the shared rules and other routes), with frame and gradient measurements. Positions are CSS pixels at 390 x 844 and 1280 x 800 unless a size is named. Revised after review round 1 (`docs/specs/scroll-cue.review.md`), and the same day after a viewing on localhost: "use the font in the signup page that we're using on the section 5, the different kind of fonts for the scroll to see more,and the back to topp, and the all families text, and the launch in, and days, hours, minutes, seconds texts as well", and of the words, "the scroll to see more is soo out of place, not in middle, somewhere odd", "place it in the bottom corner maybe, for desktop, figure it out to place it properly for phone ratio as well".

## 1. Goal

Wherever the page goes on below and only scrolling reaches it, a short line of words, "Scroll to see more", says so, at rest, beside the chevrons where there are chevrons and alone where there are none. It serves goal 3 (people reach the quiz and its form only by scrolling through seventeen screens) and the hard constraint "as magnetic on a phone as on a laptop".

## 4. Motion plan

No library: CSS transitions and class or attribute toggles, as the chevrons and the family chrome already do.

- **Show and hide.** Opacity only, 0.5 s on the chevrons' curve, cubic-bezier(0.333, 0, 0.667, 1); visibility follows at the end of a hide and switches at once on a show (the .below and .beatnav pattern), so a show fades in rather than popping after half a second. No transform, no loop, no bounce (rule 45, CHECKLIST C3). A change of place happens only while the words are hidden.
- **The rest timer.** One value, 1500 ms, for both pages: `CUE.stillMs` (src/config/cue.ts) on the landing and its twin `CUE_STILL_MS` in the family page's timing.ts (so swatch grid mode loads no chunk shared with the hero's island; a unit test keeps them equal). Any change of the scroll position, the visitor's or the page's own (the glue's settle, the beat nav's scroll, Skip quiz's ride), hides the words at once and starts the wait again. The landing reads the position in the island's frame (ride.ts, beside the logotype's tone, no listener added); the family page in its scroll handler (page.ts, one timer).
- **Landing conditions in the pin.** The words are on only when all hold: scrollY above 8 px; the first wipe at rest, not crossing (tp 0 or 1 within 0.01; the glue settles a stop inside it to an end); at or before the second wipe's start (scroll at most S2T.W2 + 0.01 screens, so the glue's own rest at 15.2 counts); section 2 built, or the scroll at or before the pull-back's end (while section 2 builds the scene holds at 5 screens and scrolling shows nothing new); and the quiz's section not yet in view (#find's top at or below the viewport's foot, read once per rest, never per frame). The tone is the logotype's rule: Urban Slate past tp 0.5. With the class, `data-at="pin"` places them on the page's edge, set while they are still hidden and dropped at the top.
- **Landing at the top.** At 8 px or less the words follow the chevrons by CSS (they are the column's next sibling). Only if the chevrons stay off there at rest, with the document going on below (the countdown script's own test, read once per rest), do the words come back alone after the same rest. Never while the loader or the intro lock the page (`html.loading`, `html.intro`: overflow hidden, the track collapsed, nothing below): the read behind "going on below" is kept until the scroll position next changes, and one taken under the lock would say nothing is below for as long as the page stays at the top (found by iteration 4's traces, 2026-11-02, as a jump back to the top inside one slow frame of the scroll away). They never copy the chevrons' forced show over the loader's poster state: on the offline soft poster they follow is-off like everywhere else.
- **Family conditions.** First-screen words: 3D path, not loading, HUD on (scroll under 0.5 screens), clear of the front case (not `html.cue-tight`), and either at the top (8 px or less, with the columns) or at rest. Story words: 3D path, not loading, HUD off, at rest, argument screen off. Since the story words need 1.5 s of rest, they never meet "Back to main page"'s 200 ms exit when the visitor scrolls back up.
- **Reduced motion.** The landing's default reduced-motion tier is the poster (no pin): the words stand with the still chevrons at the top and hide from 8 px, with the chevrons' 0.5 s opacity fade, as today's cue. On the family page reduced motion at load is grid mode (no words); turned on after load, the words show and hide at once (the page's `transition: none` list).

## 11. Decisions taken

- **D1. Over the chevrons on phones, in the bottom right corner on a laptop.** The first build set the words beside the column, on its bottom line; on viewing it read "out of place, not in middle, somewhere odd" ("place it in the bottom corner maybe, for desktop, figure it out to place it properly for phone ratio as well"). Above 1100 px the countdown holds the bottom left, so the words take the bottom right corner, the same place at the top and in the pin. Rethought on 2026-11-02 after the section index landed (it took Back to top's place at the top right, so the landing draws no Back to top): the corner stands, with the index's text edge as its reference instead of Back to top's. Both end on --inset, the index at the top right and the words at the bottom right, the countdown at the bottom left and the chevrons at the bottom centre, so the four corners of a laptop screen each hold one thing and the words meet nothing in any state. Up to 1100 px a corner beside the centred column sits within 4 px of it at 360 px wide in Plex capitals, so the words go over the column, centred, a --s1 above it: the words and the arrows pointing down read as one cue. The column's box and its bottom on --inset stay pinned (AC5.1, the v11 parity test); the countdown lifts 106 px instead of 84 to keep its labels 11 px clear of the words.
- **D2. The words come back at rest; the chevrons do not.** The chevrons' "hidden from the first scroll" is Sam's E-cue-motion. The words alone answer "keep scrolling". 1.5 s: long enough that a pause between swipes does not flicker the line, short enough to land while the visitor wonders; past it the hero's lagging camera has settled.
- **D3. One string, "Scroll", everywhere.** The brief's words; no "Swipe" (see 3).
- **D4. The sign-up's face for the chrome's small words.** The first build set the words in Open Sans (rule 4). On viewing: "use the font in the signup page that we're using on the section 5, the different kind of fonts for the scroll to see more,and the back to topp, and the all families text, and the launch in, and days, hours, minutes, seconds texts as well". So the words and the chrome they sit with take IBM Plex Mono Medium in capitals, the face of section 2's labels, the quiz, section 4 and the sign-up (E-chrome-font). The landing's first view now fetches the 2.2 KB capitals file too, preloaded.
- **D5. Urban Slate over the fog.** Better than Pearl Whisper in 22 of the 24 drawn reads (section 10), and the tone the logotype and Back to top take over the same fog. Pearl Whisper is better only where the fog turns mid-grey, as at the first wipe's end on a laptop in two reads.
- **D6. The family story's words at the bottom right, in two lines on phones.** Computed from the wash's own gradient (family.css #tint with tint.ts's pair) for all 240 shades, as the median of the words' box: at the bottom right of a phone one line of Open Sans read 4.82:1 at worst at 390 x 844; one line of the Plex capitals (E-chrome-font) reaches further into the wash's light centre and fails 45 of the 240 shades at 360 x 780 (worst 3.94:1) and 22 at 390 x 844; two lines, "SCROLL TO" over "SEE MORE", keep to the dark corner and pass every shade (worst 6.78:1 at 360 x 780, 7.10:1 at 390 x 844, 7.48:1 at 430 x 932). On the wide layout one line holds (drawn 8.86:1 and up). At the bottom left of a phone 101 of the 240 fall under 4.5:1 even in Open Sans. Urban Slate is worse everywhere there (the wash's body is dark).
- **D7. Aria-hidden.** As the chevrons they explain: the words describe a gesture, not content, and a screen reader moves by headings and links; a live region would speak at every rest.
