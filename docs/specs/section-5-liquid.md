## 4. The look and the maths

Everything here is in `src/scripts/hero/section5/jars.ts` (the renderer) and `jars-math.ts` (pure: no DOM, no three; unit tested), with `stillKey` and `stillAlt` in `src/config/signup.ts` because the DOM script needs them too. Every constant is a frozen object's field (`JAR` as it stands, `LIQ` below) and reaches a shader as a uniform or a parameter, never as a literal in a shader string (AC11). The uniforms' names below are the spec's; the build may shorten them (section 7), since the regex test reads the constants' digits, not the names.

**The body (the settled liquid).** The sand's lathe of the jar's measured interior, unchanged and byte-identical (`liquidBody(48)`, `sandBody` renamed: `JAR.inner` over the floor fan, the step, the ledge and the wall to h 0.765, then the cap's rings at r 0.371, 0.25, 0.125 and a centre, normals +y, no smoothing across the wall-to-cap edge: 482 vertices, 1,728 indices, 576 triangles, `capStart` 337, one draw), visible only while the level is above 0. No shoulder extension, no profile loop in the shader, no fixed-point clamp, no finer cap (decision D1).

*The surface is level in the jar's frame.* The vertex shader (LIQ_VERT) is the sand's clamp without the heap: uniforms `uL` (the level), `uF` (vec2: `levelY(0)` = -0.425 and the fill's range 0.695), `uR` (`JAR.innerR` 0.371), `uC` (the interior's radius at the level, `profileR(levelY(uL))`, set with the level). For every vertex yl = uF.x + uF.y x uL; a cap vertex (normal.y above 0.5) or a wall vertex above yl is put at y = yl with its xz scaled by min(1, uC / r), so the surface is a disc perpendicular to the jar's axis at the level, the band tops are planes perpendicular to the axis, the stream falls along the axis and lands at the centre as the sand's did, and nothing of the design files' plane normal, tangent, fall-direction or landing uniforms exists. Varyings `vP` (the jar-frame position after the clamp), `vN` (the view normal through `normalMatrix`), `vV` (the view vector, -mv.xyz), `vC` (1 on the cap, 0 on the wall; the cap and the wall share no vertex, so it is 0 or 1 on every fragment), each assigned on the one path.

*The dye model (LIQ_FRAG).* The body is drawn opaque (`transparent` false, `depthTest` true, `depthWrite` true, `FrontSide`, `renderOrder` 0, `frustumCulled` false) and composes the fog itself, so there is no sorting and the stream's submerged foot is hidden by the body's depth for free. The fragment, every term with its uniform:
1. The fog behind: `bg` = mix(texture2D(`tMap`, uv).rgb, `uFlat`, `uUseFlat`) with uv = gl_FragCoord.xy / `uRes` (the copy material's own four uniform objects, shared, so a still over `--night` gives the body the night as it gives the quad), decoded to linear by pow(`uY`) (the gamma, 2.2, a render parameter as built). Optional, built only if it measures under 0.1 KB gzip on a copy (section 7): the liquid's own refraction, uv offset by -n.xy x `uRf` x (1 - 0.6 ndv), `uRf` a vec2 set from the placement as the glass's `uRefract` is, at `LIQ.refract` 0.04 of the jar's screen height over the target's size.
2. The band colour C by height: hf = (vP.y - uF.x) / uF.y; c = `uK0`; c = mix(c, `uK1`, smoothstep(`uB`.x - `uE`, `uB`.x + `uE`, hf)); c = mix(c, `uK2`, the same at `uB`.y); c = mix(c, `uK3`, the same at `uB`.z). `uB` the four band tops from `layerBands(order)` (the cumulative quarters padded with 1, so a padded top changes nothing), `uK0` to `uK3` the four bands' colours in linear light (four vec3 uniforms, or one vec3[4]; set on the CPU from the order and the links' hexes, each channel held at `LIQ.floor` 1e-6 or above so pow never sees a zero base), `uE` = `LIQ.seam` 0.01: the seam is 0.02 of the fill wide, 3.0 px at 390 and 3.3 at 1280, never a hard line and never a gradient inside a band. On the cap hf = uL exactly, so the surface wears the top band's colour and crosses a seam in 72 ms as the level passes a quarter.
3. The chord d: on the wall (vC 0) d = 2 x length(vP.xz) x ndv (the view ray's chord through the lathe, the lathe's radius at that height being the fragment's own radius after the clamp); on the cap (vC 1) d = min(hL / cos t, `uD0`) with hL = vP.y - uF.x (the liquid's height under the point) and cos t = sqrt(1 - (1 - ndv x ndv) / `uEta`) the refracted cosine (`LIQ.eta2` 1.77, water); `uD0` = `LIQ.d0` 0.742 = 2 x `JAR.innerR` (held equal by the unit test). At the view's geometry the cap's ndv is about 0.19 and cos t 0.675, so a quarter's surface has d 0.257 (T = C to the power 0.35, a light tint) and a full jar's surface d0 (the shade).
4. The transmission: T = pow(C, vec3(d / uD0)) per channel. At the centre of the front wall T = C exactly, so the centre chord reproduces the shade times the fog; at the silhouette d is 0 and T is 1, the fog.
5. The scatter: S = C x (1 - lum(T)) x `uSc` x `uW`, lum the Rec. 709 weights (0.2126, 0.7152, 0.0722), `uSc` = `LIQ.scatter` 0.65 (the physical design's; the pour design's 0.25 milk floor is the lower bound if the frames read the liquid as too milky), `uW` Pearl Whisper in linear light (the light's colour as built). This is what gives the liquid a body over `--night`, so the sixteen stills show their bands, and what makes the seen colour over the fog nearly the shade itself (section 3's "seen" column).
6. col = bg x T + S. No vertical darkening multiplier of any kind: depth comes from the chord alone (decision D3).
7. On the cap only, the surface's look: the normal n perturbed as below; F = `uFr`.x + `uFr`.y x pow(1 - ndv, `uFr`.z) (`LIQ.fresnel` [0.04, 0.96, 5], the glass's constants as uniforms); r = reflect(-v, n); sky = smoothstep(`uSk`.x, `uSk`.y, r.y) (`LIQ.sky` [-0.25, 0.9]); refl = mix(`uDark`, uW, sky) (the glass's `uDark` (0.22, 0.24, 0.3) and light colour, one look for glass and liquid); col = mix(col, refl, F x `uRl`) (`LIQ.reflect` 0.85, the glass's weight); the highlight: h = normalize(`uI` + v), col += uW x pow(max(dot(n, h), 0), `uSp`.x) x `uSp`.y (`LIQ.spec` [64, 0.7]: the key as built, (0.3, 0.8, 0.55) normalised); the sheen: col += uW x F x `uSn` (`LIQ.sheen` 0.35). By the numbers at the view's geometry (the key's half-vector against a surface seen from 10 degrees above), dot(n, h) is 0.59 on the flat surface and 0.82 on a ring's steepest crest, so the highlight at power 64 adds under 1e-5 of its weight anywhere: what the eye reads on the surface is the reflection (F 0.41 at rest, so 35 percent of a mid grey, bright on a crest facing the camera and `uDark` on one facing away) and the sheen (0.14 of Pearl). The highlight is kept as the panel decided, at one pow; the rule-42 fallback of E-S5-liquid (section 11) is the highlight alone at half weight with the sheen off.
8. Out through pow(1 / uY), alpha 1.

*The ripple and the dimple (a fragment-space normal perturbation on the existing three-ring cap).* No vertex displacement, no finer cap, no z-fight with the floor fan. With rho = length(vP.xz) (the landing is the centre), e = vP.xz / max(rho, 1e-4) the radial direction, and the slope g (a tilt of the surface, rising outward when positive):
- the rings: g_r = `uRw` x `uRp`.x x sin(`uRp`.y x rho - `uRp`.z x `uT`) x exp(-`uRp`.w x rho), `uRp` = (`LIQ.rippleSlope` 0.35, k = 2 pi / `LIQ.rippleLen` 0.09 = 69.81, omega = `LIQ.rippleSpeed` 0.35 x k = 24.43 rad/s so the rings run at 0.35 jar heights a second with a ring every 0.257 s, `LIQ.rippleDecay` 5.5: 13 percent at the wall), `uRw` the ripple's weight (below), `uT` the stream's clock;
- the dimple: g_d = `uDw` x `uDm`.x x u x exp(-u x u) x (1 + `uDm`.z - `uDm`.z x u x u) with u = rho / `uDm`.y, the analytic slope of a well with a raised rim h(u) = -A exp(-u^2) (1 - c u^2): `uDm` = (2A / rho0 = 0.686 from `LIQ.dimpleDepth` 0.012 and `LIQ.dimpleR` 0.035, rho0 0.035, c = `LIQ.dimpleRim` 0.6), `uDw` the dimple's weight;
- the meniscus: g_m = (1 - smoothstep(0, `uMn`.x, uC - rho)) x `uMn`.y (`LIQ.meniscus` [0.02, 0.8]: the normal tilted towards the axis over the outer 0.02 jar heights of the cap, so the liquid reads as climbing the glass by a hair; lever 1 of section 7);
- the normal in view space: up = (0, `uTc`.x, `uTc`.y) and radial = (e.x, -e.y x `uTc`.y, e.y x `uTc`.x) (the jar's frame turned by the view's tilt, `uTc` = (cos t, sin t) of `JAR.tiltDeg`, set by `tilt(deg)` too), n = normalize(up - (g_r + g_d + g_m) x radial). The rings read through the reflection term, the dimple through the reflection and a trace of the highlight on its walls. No foam, no bubbles, no caustics at any time.

*The envelopes.* `rippleWeight(t, s)` = rise(t) x fall(t) with rise = smoothstep(s.lead, s.lead + `LIQ.rippleIn` 0.2, t) and fall = 1 - smoothstep(s.landEnd, s.total, t): 0 before the lead, 1 while the stream lands, decaying over the 0.6 s tail to exactly 0 at the total. `dimpleWeight(t, s)` = rise(t) x fall(t) x (t < s.landEnd ? 1 : exp(-(t - s.landEnd) / `LIQ.reboundDecay` 0.2) x cos(2 pi (t - s.landEnd) / `LIQ.reboundPeriod` 0.5)): one rebound after the last parcel lands (the centre bobs up to 0.29 of the depth times the tail's envelope 0.25 s after landEnd, exp(-3) = 0.0498 of it by the tail's end), exactly 0 at the total. At rest both are exactly 0, `uT` is not written (no stream, `advance()` returns before any clock), and two stills 1.5 s apart are byte-equal (AC3); under reduced motion both are 0 always.

**The stream (STREAM_VERT, STREAM_FRAG).** One tube of 16 rings by 8 sides (`tubeGeometry(16, 8)`: 128 vertices, the one attribute `position` = (cos a, s, sin a) with s 0 at the top end and 1 at the head end, 720 indices, 240 triangles; `LIQ.rings` 16, `LIQ.sides` 8), formed in its vertex shader from uniforms every frame; no sway, no rope, no bead wobble (they shimmer without MSAA; the stream is a smooth tapered column).
- The span: y = mix(`uSpan`.x, `uSpan`.y, s), `uSpan` = (top, head) from `streamSpan(t, s)`: the head falls from the source during the lead, head = max(fallY(t), yl(t) - `LIQ.sink` 0.02) with yl the surface's height now, and stays 0.02 under the surface once it has landed (the opaque body's depth hides the foot, so it shows no bulb); the top stays at the source (`LIQ.srcY` 0.62, the sand's own, 0.12 above the lip) until emit, then falls: top = t < s.emit ? srcY : fallY(t - s.emit); the span is null from landEnd (the top has reached the surface) and the mesh hidden.
- The fall: fallY(a) = srcY - v0 a - g a^2 / 2 (`LIQ.g` 6, `LIQ.v0` 0.5, the sand's formula; `grainY` renamed); the age at a height a(y) = (-v0 + sqrt(v0^2 + 2 g (srcY - y))) / g (`fallAge`); the speed v(y) = sqrt(v0^2 + 2 g (srcY - y)). The four of `uH` = (g, v0, srcY, r0).
- The radius by mass conservation: r = `uH`.w x sqrt(`uH`.y / v(y)) (`LIQ.r0` 0.03: 14.4 px wide at the source and 5.4 at the floor at 1280; 13.1 and 4.9 at 390; 8.1 px at the lip at 390, inside the neck's 0.231 by a wide margin; `streamRadius(y)` is the twin, the unit test holds it under `JAR.neckR` along the whole fall).
- The ends rounded over `LIQ.tip` 0.04 jar heights: r x sqrt(clamp((uSpan.x - y) / `uTip`.x, 0, 1)) at the top once it has left the source, r x sqrt(clamp((y - uSpan.y) / `uTip`.y, 0, 1)) at the head while it falls; `uTip` a vec2 of 0 or `LIQ.tip` per end set from `streamSpan` (the top is not rounded while it sits at the source, where the fade-in stands in; the head is not rounded once it has landed, so the foot runs into the surface whole).
- The fade-in: vA = smoothstep(0, `uFd`, srcY - y) (`LIQ.fadeIn` 0.06, as from a spout out of frame).
- The colour of a parcel by its emission time te = `uT` - a(y) through the switch chain: vK = te < `uSw`.x ? `uS0` : te < `uSw`.y ? `uS1` : te < `uSw`.z ? `uS2` : `uS3` (`uSw` a vec3 of the schedule's switches padded with a time past any window; `uS0` to `uS3` the parcels' shades in linear light, four vec3 uniforms set from the schedule's `shades` through the links' hexes); per ring, interpolated between rings into a soft seam one ring long.
- The normal the ring's radial (cos a, 0, sin a) through `normalMatrix` to vN; vV the view vector. Every varying (vK, vA, vN, vV) assigned on the one path.
- STREAM_FRAG: n, v, ndv; col = vK x (`uA` + 0.5 x uW) (lit flat, the ambient (0.42, 0.43, 0.47) and the light as built) + uW x pow(max(dot(n, h), 0), `uSt`.x) x `uSt`.y with h = normalize(uI + v) (`LIQ.streak` [16, 0.55]: one Blinn specular streak down the lit side; by the numbers the best dot(n, h) round the tube is 0.89, so power 16 gives a streak of 0.09 of Pearl at its brightest line while 32 gives 0.015 and 64 nothing; the power is pinned at 16 for that reason); alpha = `uAl`.x x vA x smoothstep(0, `uAl`.y, ndv) (`LIQ.streamAlpha` 0.85, `LIQ.aa` 0.3: the fade at the silhouette is the tube's anti-aliasing, since the full-frame targets have no MSAA); out through pow(1 / uY).
- Material: `transparent` true, `NormalBlending`, `depthTest` true, `depthWrite` false, `renderOrder` 1, `frustumCulled` false; visible only while a stream runs. `renderOrder` is set on both the body (0) and the stream (1) and pinned by the unit test, so three's transparent sort can never draw the stream before the body.
- The warm-up: the stream's program is compiled with the others in `load()` (the mesh visible at a zero-length span, `uSpan` = (srcY, srcY), `uTip` 0), so the first press on a phone compiles nothing; then hidden.

**Draw order, two targets a frame, as built.** Target A: the fog quad (`renderOrder` -10, no depth), the body (opaque, writes depth), the stream (blended, tests depth, writes none: everything of it under the surface or behind the front wall is hidden, which ends it at the landing for free); target B: the copy of A, then the glass (its own depth, `FrontSide`, `tFrame` = A), so the refraction bends the liquid, its bands, its surface and the stream as it bent the beads and the sand. Four draws at rest with liquid, three on an empty jar, five during a pour. No readbacks, no per-frame CPU work over vertices: seven uniform writes a frame during a pour (`uL`, `uC`, `uT`, `uRw`, `uDw`, `uSpan`, `uTip`) beside the placement's. Memory: the body 10.4 KB, the tube 1.5 KB of positions and 1.4 of indices, four programs (the copy, the body, the stream, the glass); freed: the 28 KB seed buffer, the palette texture, the pour's program.

**The schedule (the sand's shape and figures, with the quarter switches).** For a press of worth w (1 or 2 quarters) from level `from` to `to` = min(1, `from` + w / 4): lead = `fallTime(from)`, the positive root of fallY(a) = `levelY(from)` (the landing at the surface, no grain radius: 0.5127 s at 0, 0.4620 at a quarter, 0.4060 at a half, 0.3427 at three quarters, 0.2683 at full; the sand's 0.5116 moves by 1 ms); pour = `LIQ.perQuarter` 0.9 x w (the level reaches `to` at lead + pour, the line a quarter per 0.9 s with a `LIQ.ease` 0.15 s quadratic ease at each end inside that time, `ramp` and `pourLevel` unchanged); emit = lead + pour - `fallTime(to)` (the last parcel lands as the level reaches `to`); landEnd = lead + pour; tail = `LIQ.tail` 0.6; total = lead + pour + tail. New: a switch per quarter inside one press, `quarterSwitches(from, to, lead)`: for every quarter boundary q strictly inside (`from`, `to`), a switch at lead + (q - `from`) x 4 x 0.9 - `fallTime(q)`, the parcel that lands as the level crosses q; the press's `shades` are its follow's ids in order. Facebook or YouTube on an empty jar: 0.5127 + 0.9 + 0.6 = 2.0127 s (emit 0.9507, no switch); Instagram on an empty jar: 0.5127 + 1.8 + 0.6 = 2.9127 s (emit 1.9067, landEnd 2.3127, one switch at 0.9507, shades [vivid-violet-07, playful-pink-20]); a quarter on a half-full jar 1.9060 s (emit 0.9633); the last quarter 1.8427 s; Instagram on a half-full jar 2.8060 s (its switch at 0.9633). A press takes up to about 2.9 s on screen (E-S5-liquid, section 11). The e2e's asserts are written from these numbers with a 5 ms tolerance (AC4, AC6).

*The extension (a press during a stream).* As the sand built it: a press at tp while the stream is still emitting (tp under emit) extends it: `to` rises by the new worth; the level is rebased so it is continuous (`from`' = the level at tp, lead' = tp, the same pace, no start ease since the level is moving; a press inside the lead rebases at the lead with the ease kept, so the level never rises before the stream has reached the surface); emit' = lead' + pour' - `fallTime(to')`; total' = lead' + pour' + tail; the switches gain the old emit (the parcels emitted before it keep the old shade and land about when the level crosses the old target), then the new press's own quarter switch if its worth is two (at lead' + (old `to` + 0.25 - `from`') x 3.6 - `fallTime(old to + 0.25)`); the shades gain the new press's ids. At most two extensions (three follows): the switches array holds at most three and the shades four, and the invariant `switches.length === shades.length - 1` holds on every schedule (the unit test). The frames' example, the jar at a half, Facebook pressed and YouTube at 0.3 s (inside Facebook's 0.4060 s lead): lead' 0.4060, pour' 1.8, emit' 1.9377, total' 2.8060, switches [0.9633] (Facebook's emit), shades [blissful-blue-10, roaring-red-06]. The sand spec's example, Facebook on an empty jar and YouTube 0.8 s later: the level then 0.0708, pour' 1.5453, emit' 1.9393, total' 2.9453, switches [0.9507]. A press after the emission has ended (tp at or past emit) is queued and starts as a new stream from the level at the moment the running one reaches its total, with its own lead; the wait is at most `fallTime(0.75)` + tail = 0.9427 s. A press on a word already collected is dropped before any of this (as built). The longest one stream three quick presses can chain is 4.681 s (the unit test holds it under 5 s over every order).

**The glass.** Unchanged: `GLASS_VERT` and `GLASS_FRAG` byte for byte (the unit test pins their sha256), the material's uniforms as built, `tFrame` scene A's target.

**The tilt, as the view from above.** The body, the stream and the glass share one group: `placeJar`'s position and scale, and `rotation.x` = `JAR.tiltDeg` 10 in radians, the code as it stands. It is read now as the camera's view from a little above an upright jar: tilting the jar 10 degrees towards a level camera is the same rigid transform as pitching the camera 10 degrees down at a level jar, and the scene has no horizon, table or shadow to say otherwise; so the liquid's surface, level in the jar's frame, is the ellipse the designs described (162 x 28 px at 390, 178 x 31 at 1280), the landing, the dimple and the rings show on it, and at 0 degrees it is a 3 px sliver (the frames' pair). `placeJar(slot, view, tiltDeg)` divides the fill by the projected box's factor as built, so the box is 0.9 of the slot's height and inside the slot at any tilt (AC2 unchanged). The pour is in the jar's frame, so the stream falls along the axis and lands at the centre.
