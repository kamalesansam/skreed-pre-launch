## 4. The look and the maths

Everything here is in `src/scripts/hero/section5/jars.ts` (the renderer) and `jars-math.ts` (pure: no DOM, no three; unit tested), with `stillKey` and `stillAlt` in `src/config/signup.ts` because the DOM script needs them too. Every constant is a frozen object's field and reaches a shader as a uniform or a parameter, never as a literal in a shader string (AC11).

**The body (the settled sand).** Not particles: one small lathe mesh of the jar's measured interior, clamped at the level in the vertex shader. The interior, measured from `jar.bin` (the inward-facing rings at each height; h is the height from the bottom and r the radius, both as shares of the jar's height, the jar 1 m tall and centred as packed): the inside floor at h 0.068 (its ring at r 0.310), a fillet to the wall between 0.068 and 0.105, the straight inner wall at r 0.373 from 0.105 to 0.765, the shoulder curving in to r 0.357 at 0.802, the neck's inner radius 0.231 from 0.857 to 0.941, the lip's inner edge at r 0.257 at 1.000. `JAR.inner`, the profile the body revolves, keeps 0.002 inside the wall: `[[0.068, 0], [0.068, 0.310], [0.080, 0.345], [0.105, 0.371], [0.765, 0.371]]` (the fillet's mid-point is the build's to set inside the measured rings; the unit test checks every body vertex against the bin's inward ring radius at its height, interpolated). `sandBody(48)` gives rings on that profile (a floor fan, two fillet strips, the wall strip) and then the cap: rings at r 0.371, 0.25, 0.125 and a centre at the profile's top, normals +y, the wall's normals radial, no smoothing across the wall-to-cap edge: about 290 vertices (Float32 position and normal, 7 KB) and 1,728 indices (Uint16, 3.5 KB), 576 triangles, one draw. The body is visible only while the level is above 0 (an empty jar draws nothing; otherwise the cap would sit as a coloured disc on the floor).

*The vertex shader (SAND_VERT).* Uniforms `uLevel` (0 to 1), `uFill` (vec2: `levelY(0)` = -0.425 and the fill's range 0.695, from `JAR.floor` 0.075 and `JAR.shoulder` 0.77 as built), `uR` (`JAR.innerR` 0.371), `uCapR` (the interior's radius at the level, from `profileR`), `uCone`, `uDome` and `uConePow` (the heap, below). For every vertex y = min(y, yl) with yl = uFill.x + uFill.y x uLevel: everything above the level collapses onto the level plane. A cap vertex (normal.y above 0.5) has its radius scaled by uCapR / uR, so the cap never pokes through the foot's glass while the level is inside the fillet (the first 0.19 s of the first pour), and its height raised by the heap: y = yl + uCone x pow(1 - r01, uConePow) + uDome x q x q with r01 = r / uCapR and q = 1 - r01 x r01, with the analytic normal of that surface (the sum of the two profiles' slopes, so the cone has a lit side). Varyings vP (the jar-frame position), vN (the view normal), vV, each assigned on every path.

*The fragment shader (SAND_FRAG).* cell = ivec3(floor(vP x uG + 512)); h = hash(uvec3(cell)) (a two-multiply integer hash) giving r1 and r2; hf = (vP.y - uFill.x - uDomeRest x q x q) / uFill.y + (r1 - 0.5) x uEdge (the layer boundary follows the resting dome, the surface the next pour landed on, and is dithered by one grain: `uDomeRest` 0.012, `uEdge` 0.015); owner = hf below uBands.x ? uOwners.x : below uBands.y ? uOwners.y : below uBands.z ? uOwners.z : uOwners.w (two vec4 uniforms from `layerTops(order)`); base = texelFetch(uPalette, ivec2(int(r2 x 47.999), int(owner)), 0).rgb to linear by pow 2.2 (as the beads did); n = normalize(vN + (vec3(r1, r2, fract(r1 x 7.31)) - 0.5) x uTilt) (`uTilt` 0.4, the per-grain tilt that makes the surface granular); colour = base x (uAmbient + uLightCol x max(dot(n, uKey), 0) x 0.85), sRGB out, alpha 1; matte (no specular, no rim). The key, the ambient and the light colour are the beads' as built ((0.3, 0.8, 0.55) normalised, (0.42, 0.43, 0.47), Pearl Whisper).

*The speckle.* `uG`, the cells per jar height, is not a constant: `cells(px, dpr) = px x dpr / POUR.cellPx` with `POUR.cellPx` 2.2 device px, px the model's height on screen in CSS px (the placement's) and dpr the island's own pixel ratio (`DPR_CAP` in `src/config/hero.ts`: touch 1.25, fine 1.5), so a grain is 2.2 device px on every screen: about 124 cells at 390 x 844 on touch with the tilt (140 at 0 degrees), about 164 at 1280 x 800 on fine (184 at 0 degrees); on a DPR 3 phone whose canvas is capped at 1.25 the grain shows at about 5 physical px. No `grainMix` fade is needed, since the cell never falls under 2 device px. The cells are model-space, so the speckle moves with the jar as the page scrolls and is still when the page is (AC1's `scroll` frame; the reviewer scrubs a scroll in a browser).

*The layers.* Kept by uniforms and the palette, not by vertex colours (the cap's vertices move with the level and the boundaries fall between vertices) and not by a band texture (an upload per press for at most three bands): `layerTops(order)` gives the four band tops (the cumulative worth shares of the follows in press order, padded with 1) and the four owners (each the platform's index in `SIGNUP_FOLLOWS`, its palette row, padded with the last), set once per press, per extension and per mount. The palette: a `DataTexture` 48 x 3 RGBA8, `NearestFilter`, no mipmaps, 576 B, built once from the three links' `data-colours` in `SIGNUP_FOLLOWS` order (row 0 Facebook's 24 twice, row 1 YouTube's 24 twice, row 2 Instagram's 48).

**The pour (the only particle work, the only motion).** One `Points` mesh, `frustumCulled` false, visible only while a stream runs; its geometry has one attribute, `aSeed` (Uint8 x 4, normalised, from `grainSeeds(POUR.stream + POUR.dust, POUR.seed)`, the same on every machine) and no position attribute, so `geometry.setDrawRange(0, S + D)` is set per frame of a stream (three 0.165 draws nothing from a geometry with neither a position nor an index unless the range is finite) and set to 0 when none runs; its draw is one, after the body, in scene A (the fog quad, the body, the points), so the glass pass in scene B refracts the stream as it refracted the beads. Everything a grain does is a function of the uniforms, `gl_VertexID` and its seed: no per-frame CPU work over the grains, no readbacks, no per-grain state anywhere.

*The source and the stream.* Grain k (k below S) is emitted at te = (k + jitter) / rate, a fixed grid in the stream's own clock (rate = `POUR.streamPerQuarter` / `POUR.perQuarter`, 1,536 per 0.9 s, about 1,707 a second, the jitter the seed's first byte over one slot), on while te is under the window's end `uWin.x` and under the clock `uT`; its age a = uT - te. The emission grid is fixed per grain so that extending the window (below) turns on more grains later and moves nothing in the air. Its place at the source: theta from the seed, rs = `POUR.streamR` x (0.3 + 0.7 r.z x r.z) (dense at the centre), xz = (cos theta, sin theta) x (rs + (r.w - 0.5) x `POUR.spread` x max(a, 0)); the source is on the jar's axis at `POUR.srcY` 0.62 (jar units from the centre: 0.12 above the lip at +0.5), fading in over the first 0.06 of fall (alpha = smoothstep(0, 0.06, srcY - y)).

*The fall.* y = srcY - v0 a - g a x a / 2 (`POUR.v0` 0.5, `POUR.g` 6 jar heights per second squared: a slow, readable fall, not a real jar's; the stream reaches the floor of an empty jar in 0.512 s). `grainY(a)` in `jars-math.ts` is the shader's twin.

*The landing and the slide.* ys = uFill.x + uFill.y x uLevel + the heap's height at the grain's radius (the surface under it now); d = ys - y (the depth under the surface); landed while 0 below d below `POUR.sink` 0.25: the grain is held at y = ys + grainR, its xz scaled by 1 + 0.6 d / sink (the slide outward along the heap), alpha 1 - smoothstep(0.5, 1, d / sink); off (gl_Position outside the clip volume, size 0) when d is past sink, which at the landing speed is about 80 ms after touching down. gl_PointSize = clamp(2 x `POUR.grainR` x uPx, 1, 6) with uPx the model's height in device px: 2.2 device px at 390 on touch with the tilt (2.5 at 0 degrees), 2.9 at 1280 on fine (3.2 at 0 degrees), the sand's own grain.

*The heap: a cone settling to the dome.* While a stream lands the cap carries the fidelity design's cone, uCone x pow(1 - r01, uConePow) with `POUR.conePow` 1.4 and the peak `conePeak` = `JAR.innerR` x tan(`POUR.coneDeg`) (12 degrees of mean slope: 0.079 jar heights, 17 px at 390 with the tilt), rising from 0 to the peak over `POUR.moundRise` 0.25 s after the lead, held while the stream lands, then over the tail eased to the resting dome, uDome x q x q with `POUR.moundRest` 0.012 (3 px at 390), over `POUR.moundSettle` 0.6 s (the tail). Both heights are scaled by min(1, (the shoulder's y - yl) / conePeak) so the heap never rises past the shoulder at a full jar. `moundAt(t, s)` returns both uniforms.

*The dust.* Mote k (k from S) is born at tb = `lead0` + (k - S + jitter) / dustRate on the same fixed grid (`POUR.dustRate` 1,024 motes per 2.7 s, about 379 a second), on while tb is under the landing window's end `uWin.z` (the level's end, where the last grains land) and under the clock, alive for life = clamp(0.5 + 0.7 r.y, to the stream's total) seconds, u = a / life; e = (1 - exp(-3 a)) / 3 (the drag); xz = (cos, sin)(r.z x 2 pi) x (0.03 + 0.05 r.w + (0.05 + 0.15 r.y) x e) clamped inside r 0.34; y = the surface + (0.1 + 0.25 r.w) x e - 0.05 x max(0, a - 0.4), clamped under 0.45 (the lip is at 0.5); alpha = smoothstep(0, 0.1, u) x (1 - smoothstep(0.6, 1, u)) x `POUR.dustAlpha` 0.6 (pending Sam under rule 42, section 11: opaque motes, depth-tested with no blending, are the default if he or the reviewer reads the alpha as a glow); gl_PointSize = (`POUR.dustPx`.x + `POUR.dustPx`.y x r.x) x uDpr, 1.2 to 2.0 device px, so a mote reads on a phone at the island's real pixel ratio (the design's 1 to 1.8 px at alpha 0.35 measured under 1.3 px there). About 320 motes alive at a time. Their colour: the family of the grains landing when they are born (the owner chain below applied at tb - lead0). The dust is Sam's word ("dust particles, like sand") and is never the cut that saves the budget line (section 7).

*The colour of a grain.* Varyings vS (the shade column, r.z x 47.999) and vA (the alpha); the owner by the grain's emission time against the switches: te below uSw.x ? uOw.x : te below uSw.y ? uOw.y : uOw.z (the owners' palette rows; a switch that does not exist is set past the window). Fragment POUR_FRAG: texelFetch(uPalette, ivec2(int(vS), int(owner)), 0) to linear, lit flat by uAmbient + 0.5 x uLightCol, sRGB out with alpha vA; the material transparent, depth test on, depth write off, normal blending (the stream and the dust; the opaque default of section 11 turns blending off for the motes alone). Any grain or mote that is off writes gl_Position = vec4(2, 2, 2, 1) and size 0, and assigns every varying on that path too (ANGLE's uninitialised-variable warning, graft m).

*The durations.* For a press of worth w (1 or 2 quarters) from level `from` to `to` = `from` + w / 4: lead = `fallTime(from)` (the positive root of grainY(a) = levelY(from) + grainR: 0.512 s at 0, 0.461 at a quarter, 0.405 at a half, 0.341 at three quarters); pour = `POUR.perQuarter` 0.9 x w (the level reaches `to` at lead + pour, the line a quarter per 0.9 s with a `POUR.ease` 0.15 s quadratic ease at each end inside that time); emit = lead + pour - `fallTime(to)` (the last grain lands as the level reaches `to`); tail = `POUR.tail` 0.6; total = lead + pour + tail. Facebook or YouTube on an empty jar: 0.512 + 0.9 + 0.6 = 2.012 s (emit 0.951); Instagram on an empty jar: 0.512 + 1.8 + 0.6 = 2.912 s (emit 1.907); a quarter on a half-full jar 1.905 s; the last quarter 1.841 s. A press takes up to about 2.9 s on screen (E-S5-pour, section 11).

*The extension (a press during a stream).* A press at tp (in the stream's clock) while the stream is still emitting (tp under emit) extends it: `to` rises by the new worth; the level is rebased so it is continuous (`from`' = the level at tp, lead' = tp, pour' = (`to`' - `from`') x 0.9 / 0.25, the same pace, with no start ease since the level is moving; the unit test checks continuity, not smoothness: the slope changes by under a fifth at the press); emit' = lead' + pour' - `fallTime(to')`; total' = lead' + pour' + tail; the switches gain the old emit (the grains emitted before it are the old family's and land about when the level crosses the old target) and the owners the new platform's row; the dust's window end moves to the new landing end. At most two extensions (three follows). Example, Facebook on an empty jar and YouTube 0.8 s later: the level at 0.8 s is 0.071, `to`' 0.5, pour' 1.544, emit' 1.939, total' 2.944 s, switches [0.951], owners [0, 1]. A press after the window has closed (tp at or past emit: the last grains falling, the level in its last fall time, or the tail) is queued (`queue`) and starts as a new stream from the level at the moment the running one reaches its total, with its own lead; the wait is at most `fallTime(to)` + tail, about 1.1 s. A press on a word already collected is dropped before any of this (as built). If the budget line cannot carry the extension (section 7), every press during a stream is queued and this paragraph's first half is struck in the build record.

*The buffer.* `POUR.stream` 5,632 and `POUR.dust` 1,536 vertices (the longest one stream, three presses each at the last emitting moment, emits for about 3.1 s at 1,707 a second and lands for about 3.4 s at 379 a second; the unit test computes both bounds by chaining the schedules and holds the two counts above them): 7,168 seeds, 28 KB on the GPU, uploaded once. `S` = min(`POUR.stream`, ceil(uWin.x x rate)) and `D` = min(`POUR.dust`, ceil((uWin.z - lead0) x dustRate)) set the draw range each frame of a stream.

**The glass.** Unchanged: `GLASS_VERT` and `GLASS_FRAG` byte for byte (the unit test pins their sha256), the material's uniforms as built, `tFrame` scene A's target so the refraction bends the sand, the layers and the stream as it bent the beads; two targets a frame as built. The pour's program is compiled in the chunk's warm-up with the others (the points visible with a draw range of 0 during the compile, then hidden), so the first press never compiles a program on a phone.

**The tilt and the placement.** The body, the points and the glass share one group: `placeJar`'s position and scale, and `rotation.x` = `JAR.tiltDeg` 10 in radians (top towards the viewer), one constant, pending Sam (section 11; the review frames carry a pair at 0 degrees). With a tilt the projected box of the jar's bounding box is taller than the model (cos t + `JAR.width` x sin t of its height, 1.1235 at 10 degrees), so `placeJar(slot, view, tiltDeg)` divides the fill by that factor: the projected box is 0.9 of the slot's height and inside the slot at any tilt, the model's height 0.9 of the slot's at 0 degrees exactly as built (AC2 as reworded by the panel: "the jar inside its slot at 0.9 of its height, its screen box within the slot"). At 390 x 844 the slot is 218 x 273 and the model 218.7 px tall (245.7 at 0 degrees); at 1280 x 800, 240 x 300 and 240.3 (270). The pour is in the jar's frame, so with the tilt the stream leans with the jar and lands at its centre.

**The constants.** `JAR` (frozen): `fill` 0.9, `width` 0.79861, `floor` 0.075, `shoulder` 0.77, `fovDeg` 28, `distance` 6, `refract` 0.12 (all as built); new: `tiltDeg` 10, `innerR` 0.371, `inner` (the profile above), `profile` (the clamp's profile: `inner` plus the shoulder's measured points [0.802, 0.355] and [0.857, 0.229], kept 0.002 inside), `neckR` 0.231 (the unit test's bound on the stream); gone: `beadR`, `band`, `perJar`, the old `innerR` 0.92. `POUR` (frozen): `g` 6, `v0` 0.5, `srcY` 0.62, `streamR` 0.025, `spread` 0.08, `grainR` 0.004, `sink` 0.25, `perQuarter` 0.9, `ease` 0.15, `tail` 0.6, `coneDeg` 12, `conePow` 1.4, `moundRest` 0.012, `moundRise` 0.25, `moundSettle` 0.6, `streamPerQuarter` 1536, `dustRate` 379.26 (1024 / 2.7), `stream` 5632, `dust` 1536, `cellPx` 2.2, `edge` 0.015, `tilt` 0.4, `dustAlpha` 0.6, `dustPx` [1.2, 0.8], `seed` 29. The uniforms' names are the build's and may be shortened (section 7); the regex test reads the constants' digits, not the names.

- **The interior (4).** `jar.bin`'s interior is a step, not a fillet: the floor's ring at r 0.310 rises vertically to a ledge at h 0.105 that runs out to the wall at r 0.373, so `JAR.inner` is `[[0.07, 0], [0.07, 0.308], [0.107, 0.308], [0.107, 0.371], [0.765, 0.371]]` and `JAR.profile` adds the shoulder's measured points `[0.802, 0.355]` and `[0.857, 0.229]`, every point 0.002 inside the inward rings the unit test reads from the bin; the cap's clamp widens from r 0.308 to 0.371 as the level passes h 0.107, about 0.17 s into the first pour.
- **The copy ids (3).** `still` collided with the form's "Still trying...", so the jar's descriptions are `jarEmpty` and `jarLayers`; the words are this spec's.
- **The buffer (4).** The motes come first (ids 0 to 1535) and the grains after, so extending a stream moves no mote in the air; `POUR.stream` is 6656, not 5632, since three presses each at the last emitting moment chain an emission window of about 3.79 s (6,472 grains), the unit test holding the buffer above the chained bound over every order; the longest chained stream totals about 4.66 s, under 5 s; `POUR.dust` 1536 holds.
- **A press inside the lead (4).** It rebases at the lead with the start ease kept, so the level never rises before the stream reaches the surface; AC6's example therefore totals 2.805 s, asserted within 0.15 s of the formula's 2.7. A stream's rise is the actual rise times 3.6 s, so a press clamped at full lasts no longer than its rise.
- **The constants in the shaders (4, AC11).** The dust's shape numbers are a frozen `DUST` object reaching `POUR_VERT` as four vec4 uniforms, `POUR` gains `slide`, `fadeIn` and `grainPx`, and the gamma is a uniform, because the spec's literals 0.25, 0.4, 0.6 and 2.2 are also `POUR` values and the no-literal test would read them as baked; the test skips 0, 0.5, 1 and 2. The sand shaders carry `vP` and `vN` and no `vV` (the matte fragment has no use for it). The body is 482 vertices (flat rings per strip) and 576 triangles.
- **The palette (3).** The texels are the hex bytes of the links' `data-colours`, the gamma applied once in the shader, so the sand is lighter than the beads, which three's `Color` had converted twice.
- **The hooks (6).** `still(order, level?)`, `scrub(t)` and `tilt(deg)` optional on the island's type, folded with `hooks()` behind `import.meta.env.PUBLIC_HERO_HOOKS === '1'` (lever 1); shorter uniform names and tight GLSL (lever 2); the production chunk carries the word `scrub` twice as the stream state's field name, not the hook (the reviewer decides on the name).
- **The budget (7).** The orchestrator's budget note: the shader strip outside the section, the jar chunk's line 9.5; on the production build with the stills the enforced total reads 248.1 of 250 (the stills' `import.meta.glob` map costs about 1.2 KB in the DOM script, 4.0 of 6, against the estimate of 0.6).
- **The strip and the hero (7, found by the evidence step).** Two textual searches on the site's own shader strings, `section3/ground.ts`'s encode line and `post/shaders.ts`'s `withCover`, did not match the strip's spacing: the first threw at `startHero` and every build with the strip fell to the poster tier, the second left the seam cloud out of the composite. Both now match with or without the spaces. The dev server of the day served an older strip (its output broke four of the island's programs) because a change to `glsl-strip.mjs` reaches it only after a restart, so the frames, the stills, the contrast and the flash sweep were rendered from the served test build (`dist-s5t` under wrangler), not from the dev server.
- **The frames and the stills (3, 8).** Twenty frames a width (the 0 degree pair, the scrubbed pour at 0.15, 0.55 and 1.40 s, the settled jar, the second press at 1.0 s, the full jar, the scroll, the poster tier before and after a press, the seams); `frames.mjs` opens the poster tier with the gate's override and renders its two frames alone with `--poster`. The 64 stills: the largest 34,144 B (`jar-yif-2x.webp`), every one under its cap; 831 KB in `src/assets/signup/stills` in all, lazy, the poster tier alone.
- **The contrast (AC9).** 50 lines at both widths, medians 2.09:1 to 3.17:1, all under 4.5:1 (E-S5-contrast, pending); `contrast.mjs` now corrects its scroll once the page has settled, since the jump from the hall lands 284 px low at 390.
- **The flash sweep (5).** One Instagram pour at 0.1 s steps at 390 x 844 passes the rule (two turns of a region in a second, of six allowed), run through a port of `loop-flash.py`'s rule because that script's Pillow is not on this machine; the whole frame's slow luminance swing is the fog's, not the pour's.
- **The suites (AC12).** The eight page-level suites 53 of 54 with the room's AC14 case passing alone on a rerun (the hall's loading voice, the machine's timing); the quiz suite 58 of 67 with the six machine failures of 2026-10-28, a seventh ride case of the same kind, the pictures' keyboard case on its timing, and one real finding that predates the sand: at the quiz's result the unlock opens section 5, whose lines stand in Chrome's accessibility tree as their raw text in capitals until its DOM script mounts within a screen of it, so the email field is named "EMAIL" (the quiz writes its two copies at build; section 5 at mount). Left for the review.
- **Not verified in a browser by a person.** The frames are the machine's (headless Chrome on this machine's GPU); nothing was checked on a real phone, and whether the 2.2 device px grain reads as sand at a phone's pixel ratio is for the review.
