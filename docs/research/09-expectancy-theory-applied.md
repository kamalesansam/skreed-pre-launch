# Expectancy theory applied to the teaser

Source Sam pointed at: positivepsychology.com/expectancy-theory (the "three components" section). The page is blocked from the build sandbox, so this is written from Vroom's original formulation (Victor Vroom, *Work and Motivation*, 1964) and the Porter-Lawler extension, which is what that article summarises.

## The theory in one line

People put effort into an action when they believe three things at once:

**Motivation = Expectancy × Instrumentality × Valence**

| Component | The question in the user's head | Zero if |
|---|---|---|
| **Expectancy** (effort → performance) | "If I try, can I actually do this?" | The step looks hard, slow, unclear, or likely to fail (a long form, a permission prompt, a broken link). |
| **Instrumentality** (performance → outcome) | "If I do it, will I really get the thing?" | The reward feels vague, delayed, conditional, or untrustworthy ("we'll let you know"). |
| **Valence** (value of the outcome) | "Do I even want the thing?" | The reward is generic, not for me, or discount-shaped when I wanted status or identity. |

Because it is a product, one zero kills the whole thing. A huge reward with a confusing form converts as badly as an easy form with no reward.

## Why the saved Instagram posts convert

Every "comment DESIGN and I'll DM the link" reel in Sam's collections is expectancy theory in miniature:
- Expectancy is maximal: typing one word is effortless and obviously works.
- Instrumentality is explicit: "I'll send it to you", with a specific noun (the repo, the 42 skills, the PDF).
- Valence is pre-sold by the video: you've just watched the thing work.

That is the bar for every ask on skreed.in.

## Applied to the three asks

### 1. Reserve your shade (phone number + optional email)

| Component | Current plan | Fix or confirm |
|---|---|---|
| Expectancy | One required field (+91 phone), no OTP, Turnstile invisible. | Good. Add: show the field pre-focused on the shade card with the shade already chosen, so the user sees it is one tap. Show "takes 10 seconds" nowhere; show it by making it true. |
| Instrumentality | "Mauve is yours. First dibs Nov 1." | Make the outcome concrete and immediate: the confirmation screen shows the reserved shade, the user's queue position, and the exact date and hour they get access. Send the WhatsApp confirmation within a minute so the promise is proven before they leave. |
| Valence | Priority access + free shipping first 48 h. | Priority is status, which fits the brand voice. Strengthen it with identity: the reserved shade carries their name on the share card ("Reserved by Priya · Mauve 07"). Avoid discount framing; a discount lowers perceived valence for a design-led brand. |

### 2. Follow @skreedofficial

| Component | Current plan | Fix or confirm |
|---|---|---|
| Expectancy | Universal link to the profile; broadcast-channel join link. | In Instagram's in-app browser the follow is one tap, which is why the bio-link audience is the easy case. On desktop, show the handle as copyable text, not just a link. |
| Instrumentality | Honour-system "I followed" to reveal the 241st shade. | Weak link in the chain: the user cannot see the reward until after an unverifiable action. Flip it: reveal the 241st shade *in the broadcast channel* so joining the channel (which forces the follow) is the step that delivers the reward. Instrumentality becomes certain. |
| Valence | A hidden shade; first look at drops. | Good for Colour-Matchers. Add a second valence for Minimalists: launch-day stock alerts for their device, only in the channel. |

### 3. Share (card to WhatsApp or Stories)

| Component | Current plan | Fix or confirm |
|---|---|---|
| Expectancy | Native share sheet with a pre-rendered image. | One tap. Keep the card pre-generated so there is no wait after the tap. |
| Instrumentality | Shade Twin: when a friend reserves, both unlock a pair shade and move up. | Show the progress: "1 of 3 friends joined" with the pair shade shown greyed, then coloured in. Delayed, invisible rewards have near-zero instrumentality. |
| Valence | Pair shade + queue jump. | The card itself must be worth posting on its own: a beautiful object with the user's shade and name. The reward is secondary to looking good on Stories. |

## Design rules that fall out of this

1. Every ask shows its reward before the action, not after.
2. Every reward is specific (a named shade, a date, a position), never "updates".
3. Every action is one tap or one field, and visibly so.
4. Progress toward delayed rewards is always on screen.
5. Status and identity rewards over price rewards, which also matches the brand rule against discount language.
6. Prove the first promise fast: the confirmation WhatsApp within a minute is what makes every later promise believable.
