1. Page in one paragraph:
A French energy-drink launch site built in Webflow, with a hand-written three.js scene behind the DOM. A fixed full-viewport WebGL canvas holds one can model (cloned 24 times on desktop, 12 on mobile) and a two-part chrome podium. The page is eleven scroll spacers (125svh each) under fixed text overlays, and one GSAP timeline with one tween per section is seeked directly by the Lenis scroll position, so every section boundary is a fixed 3D keyframe: a wave carousel of six flavours, a giant tilted hero can, the back label scanned by a masked spotlight for four benefits, a "ZERO BULLSHIT" poster beat over a flavour smoke video, a twisted line-up of every can, the cans flying out and back around the FAQ, then a seamless loop to the top. Wheel input is paged a section at a time (1.5 s or 1 s, cubic ease-out); the carousel drags, snaps and recolours the whole page per flavour. Opens with a 7 s logo video and a 4.5 s 3D intro. About 5.0 MB first load.

What happens, step by step, with timings and easing:
1. Scroll mechanics: Lenis (smooth wheel, lerp 0.1, infinite, syncTouch) runs from the scene's own rAF. On sections 1 to 7 a wheel listener cancels native scroll and calls lenis.scrollTo(next section top minus 10 px), easing 1 - (1 - t)^3, locked until it lands (one notch = one section; extra notches are ignored). Duration is 1.5 s out of sections 1, 2, 6 and 7 and 1 s between benefits. After section 7 the wheel scrolls freely. On desktop, when scrolling stops (250 ms debounce, or scrollend plus 50 ms) it snaps back to section 1 or 2 if one of them is nearest.
2. The choreography: one paused GSAP timeline (default ease power1.inOut) with one tween per section, each lasting section height / total height. Every frame calls timeline.seek(scroll / total), so the 3D state is a pure function of scroll position, with no extra smoothing beyond Lenis and the paging tween. Keyframes reached at each section top:
   - 2 Profile: camera z 6, FOV 40; can at (0.5, -0.5), rotated x -37.5, y 15, z 22.5 deg; spacing 3.5 (neighbours pushed off screen); wave 0; podium opens to 3; key lights 30.
   - 3 Benefit 1: camera (0, -2, 12), pitch 10, roll -10 deg, FOV 20; can y -0.8, label spun 120 deg (back to camera); spacing 2.2; key lights 0, tint 0.2 (can almost black); spotlight 35 at height 2.2; pointer influence 0.
   - 4, 5, 6 Benefits 2 to 4: can y -0.48, 0.02, 0.5, so each next printed line rises into the fixed light band; roll 5, -10, 5 deg; spin 130, 120, 130 deg.
   - 7 Argument: camera (0, 0, 8), FOV 45; can z -0.5, rotated x -20, z -5 deg, spin 0; spacing 5; key lights 45, cone 1.5x, tint 2; spotlight off; pointer influence 0.2.
   - 8 Full range: camera (-3, -3.5, 20) rotated 10, -9, -10 deg, FOV 30; spacing 0.47 (cans nearly touching); swirl 1 (each can pitched by x * 0.06 * window ratio + 0.2, so the line twists); podium pushed 20 away; key lights 10, cone 3x; tint 2; swipe speed 2.
   - 9 FAQ: camera (0, -5.5, 10), FOV 30, spacing 0.6, lights 0 (cans exit upward). At the FAQ top the camera jumps to (0, 8, 10), FOV 20, spacing 5.

3. Per can, every frame: slot x = wrap(i * 3.5 - carouselPosition) around the ring of cans; centredness p = 1 - |x| / 3.5 (1 only for the centre can); position x = x * spacing, y = sin(x * spacing * waveAmount) where waveAmount = 0.25 x window ratio (1440 / width, clamped 1 to 2.4) x (1 - progress out of section 1) x wave, z = -|x| - 0.2 (recede). Everything blends to the keyframe values by progress out of section 1. Only the centre can gets the label spin (child mesh rotation z = can rotation y * 0.6 + spin * p) and the pointer tilt (rotation y += cursorX / 1280 * 0.2 * p, rotation x += cursorY / 1280 * 0.2 * p; cursor smoothed at 10/s; not centred, so it is about 7 deg at screen middle and 13 deg at the right edge on 1512 px).
4. Lighting is the storytelling device. Spot 1 above (0, 3.5, 0) and spot 2 below (0, -3, 2) are the key lights, with intensity and cone keyed per section. Spot 3 sits at (0, height, 2) aiming 2.5 below and projects a gobo (a soft white crescent on black) onto the can. It is only on for the benefits, so as the can rises the crescent highlight reads one printed line at a time while the environment light is tinted down to 0.2.
5. Carousel (section 1): arrows set target +-3.5; position eases exponentially at 10/s (42% at 50 ms, 89% at 200 ms, 99% at 400 ms). Drag moves 0.02 units per px (175 px per can) with grab and grabbing cursors; on release the last movement is multiplied by 8 as a fling, then it snaps to the nearest can. Clicking a side can steps once; clicking the centre can pages to section 2 (1.5 s). When the centred index changes: name and description swap (fade 0.5 s power2.inOut; characters mask out and back in, yPercent 110 to 0, 0.6 s power3.out, 0.01 s stagger, 0.3 s delay); pagination dot slides (0.6 s power3.out; on wrap it shrinks out in 0.3 s and back in 0.45 s); a conic colour ring rotates 60 deg (0.8 s power2.inOut); the two page colour variables cross-fade over 0.6 s (registered @property colours), repainting the radial wash, the tagline fill and the loop video choice; a tick sound plays.
6. DOM layer (ScrollTrigger per section): fixed containers fade with autoAlpha 0.5 s power2.inOut. Text uses SplitText masks (in: yPercent 110 to 0, 0.6 to 0.7 s power3.out, stagger 0.08 per line or 0.01 per character; out: to -110). Benefits start revealing at 0.35 s and draw a strike line through the bad-ingredient tag over 0.8 s (power3.out) after 1 s. Argument letters pop from scale 0.6 to 1 (0.5 s back.out(2), 0.04 s stagger, 0.4 s delay) and their glow fades in at 1 s. The loop video cross-fades in 0.6 s. Sounds play on leaving the hero and on each benefit change.

Code excerpt (rewritten from the inline module, not verbatim; values exact):
  const lenis = new Lenis({ autoRaf: false, infinite: true, syncTouch: true });
  const tl = gsap.timeline({ paused: true, defaults: { ease: 'power1.inOut' } });
  KEYFRAMES.forEach((k, i) => tl.to(state, { ...k, duration: sections[i].h / scrollHeight }));
  function frame(t, dt) {
    lenis.raf(t);
    const pos = wrap(lenis.animatedScroll, 0, scrollHeight - viewportHeight);
    tl.seek(pos / scrollHeight);                        // scroll position is the playhead
    carousel.position = lerp(carousel.position, carousel.target, dt * 10);
    cans.forEach((can, i) => {
      const x = wrap(i * 3.5 - carousel.position, minX, maxX);
      const p = clamp(1 - Math.abs(x) / 3.5, 0, 1);       // 1 = centred can
      can.position.set(x * state.spacing, Math.sin(x * state.spacing * waveAmt), (-Math.abs(x) - 0.2) * state.wave);
      can.children.forEach(m => { m.rotation.z = can.rotation.y * 0.6 + state.canSpin * p; });
    });
    spot3.intensity = state.spotIntensity;              // gobo light that reads the label
    spot3.position.set(0, state.spotY, 2);
    composer.render();
  }
  addEventListener('wheel', e => {                      // paging on sections 1 to 7
    e.preventDefault(); if (locked || Math.abs(e.deltaY) < 4) return;
    const slow = [0, 1, 5, 6].includes(current);
    lenis.scrollTo(tops[current + Math.sign(e.deltaY)] - 10, { duration: slow ? 1.5 : 1, easing: t => 1 - Math.pow(1 - t, 3), lock: true });
  }, { passive: false });

9. What makes it work, and what would be lost without WebGL or a cursor:
There is exactly one object, and scroll only ever moves the camera, the object and the lights between hand-set keyframes, one per section. Paging means the user always lands on a composed frame. The best trick is using the real packaging as content: the benefits section adds no graphics; it turns the can round, kills the fill light and slides the printed benefit list through a crescent spotlight while the DOM repeats each line in big type. Colour does the rest: one pair of registered CSS colour variables per flavour drives the wash, the tagline fill and the video choice, cross-faded in 0.6 s, so picking a flavour repaints the site. It is cheap: 5 MB, and one 163 KB can reused 24 times. Weak spots: 7 s of video plus a 4.5 s intro before scroll unlocks, wheel hijacking with a lock, and a pointer tilt that is not centred. Without WebGL, the DOM layer survives (text reveals, washes, videos, tagline). Because the 3D path is a fixed pose per section, it can be rebuilt as pre-rendered sequences scrubbed by scroll (one per transition, ten transitions). You lose live lighting, free carousel drag physics and per-flavour label swaps (they need a sequence per colourway or a tintable render). Without a cursor, only the can tilt and the grab cursors are lost; swipe paging and carousel drag work by touch.
