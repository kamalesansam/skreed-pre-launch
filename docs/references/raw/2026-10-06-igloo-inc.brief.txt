What Sam liked, in Sam's words: the scroll animations and how the assets are used for the animations
Where it is on the page: the whole experience. The site is one full-screen WebGL canvas with no DOM page underneath; "scroll" is a virtual value that drives three looping 3D scenes.

1. Page in one paragraph:
igloo.inc is a single full-screen WebGL2 app (three.js r143 in a custom engine; the console credits the studio abeto) that replaces the page entirely: no native scroll, no DOM text, every word drawn in WebGL as MSDF text. Wheel, arrow keys and drag move one virtual scroll value that loops forever through three scenes: a misty snow mountain with an igloo built from 70 separate glowing ice blocks (manifesto), three refractive ice cubes each holding a portfolio company logo (click opens a detail view), and a vertical dive through glowing rings into a room where 150,000 particles form social-network logos. Each scene scrubs its own camera timeline, scenes are joined by a full-screen ice-crack wipe with chromatic aberration, and 1.4 s after input stops the scroll glides to the nearest resting pose. Cold grey-blue palette, IBM Plex Mono throughout, optional sound design. About 17.4 MB, all loaded behind the preloader.

3. The thing Sam liked, in detail:
Resting state: after the intro the scroll sits at 0.66 (igloo timeline at 49.5%). Camera about (-13.5, 2.65, 14.4) looking at (0, 1.24, 0), FOV 30, mouse parallax up to 0.07 / 0.025, shake 0.01. Blocks idle-breathe (each pushed along its centroid by a slow sine of time and x). All UI text visible, sound off.
Trigger: wheel (deltaY x 0.00075 units), ArrowDown/ArrowUp (0.1125 units a press), mouse or touch drag (1.25 units per full screen height), autopilot after 1.4 s idle. There is no native scroll.
What happens, step by step, with timings and easing:
1. Input to motion: input adds to a target. Each frame a first follower chases it at 7.5% per frame (60 fps normalised, capped at 0.075 units, about 100 px, per frame), a second follower chases that at 15% per frame, and the target may never lead by more than 0.5625 units. One 100 px wheel notch moves 0.075 units and settles in about 0.5 s: heavy, damped, no overshoot. Speed feeds a velocity value (friction 0.98 per frame) used by shaders and the cube FOV kick (45 deg falling to 40 deg at full speed).
2. Scene scrubbing: the loop is igloo 2.35 + cubes 3 + entry 5.5 units. The screen is a 1-unit window; the scene under its top is rendered, plus the next scene while the window straddles a boundary. Each scene owns a paused GSAP timeline set to progress = (position + 1 - sceneTop) / (sceneHeight + 1), so camera moves are keyframed in timeline time and scrubbed 1:1 by scroll.
3. Igloo camera (21-unit timeline): camera y descends to 2.5 and target y to 1 over the first 14 units (power2.out); from unit 7 to 21 x goes -13.25 to -15.25 and z 13.25 to 23.25 (power1.inOut). Coming in from the previous scene (progress 0 to 0.4) the upper blocks are blown out along their centroids by up to 2x with random spins (sine.in falloff), so scrolling on from the room shows the igloo assembling. The baked lighting cross-fades from an assembled lightmap to an exploded lightmap as each block separates (mix = 5 x displacement).
4. Scene transition, across the 1-unit overlap: a full-screen shader mixes scene A and scene B with a diagonal edge (slope 0.2 x aspect). The red channel of a 1024 px data texture breaks the edge into ice-crack shapes, green adds blocky tech displacement (0.025 UV), blue wobbles the diagonal. Scene A slides up by 0.4 x progress squared while B rises from 0.4 below. A 5-tap chromatic aberration peaks along the cut and fades toward the screen edges, dithered with blue noise. Mid-overlap the frame is about half each scene.
5. Cubes: cube centres are 1 unit apart and the camera drops 5.75 world units per cube. As a cube nears centre its HUD draws on: leader lines in 0.2 s (linear), text in 0.4 s with a glyph scramble, a beep. Each cube renders twice a frame (back faces into a texture, then front faces sampling it) to fake refraction against an EXR environment with normal and roughness maps.
6. Auto-centre: 1.4 s after the last input. If straddling two scenes it tweens to that scene's rest pose (2 s plus extra, the bundle's named in-out ease "inOut3"); inside the cubes it snaps to the nearest cube (1.6 to 2.4 s); inside the entry scene it glides to the room (4 s per unit, clamped 2 to 20 s).
7. Entry dive (9.2-unit timeline): camera y 0 to -9.83 (custom ease, 7 units); the up vector rolls 180 deg (power3.inOut, 5.25 units) and then eases back to upright; FOV 22 to 30; z pulls back to -3 at the end. Each ring hides once passed. At each pass (timeline 2.0, 2.95, 3.8) glitch squares flash in the composite (0.5 s in power1.in, 0.4 s out power1.out) and a portal hum peaks. Particles fade in from 1.5, floor from 3.4 (power2.out), text cylinders from 4.5.
8. Links room: arrows and selector show between 64% and 90% of the entry timeline. Next or previous (click a screen half, ArrowLeft/Right, or a swipe over 100 px faster than 1000 px/s) swaps the 3D volume texture the particles sample, fires a noise burst 1 to 0 over 0.5 s (power2.inOut), resets the sculpture rotation and spins the floor rings 4 units over 3 s (power4.out). The pointer drives a 128 px fluid sim (splat radius 0.22, force 35) that stirs the particles.
9. Project open (click a cube): scroll locks; the cube centres (up to 1.5 s); camera dollies 3.5 units in (1.25 s power3.in) while the composite pushes frost displacement and RGB split (1.25 s power3.in), then reveals the detail scene (1.25 s sine.out, starting 0.75 s later); detail camera z 4 to 2.5 over 2 s; text blocks reveal in sequence (0.25 s short lines, 0.75 s paragraphs, 0.1 to 0.3 s apart). Close reverses in 1.25 s (power2.out); scroll returns after 1 s.
Hover in the hero: blocks within 1 to 3 units of the projected pointer push outward (up to about 0.5 to 0.8), smoothed by two followers (5% and 6% per frame). The five nearest displaced blocks get white 25% lines and two-digit distance labels (in 0.1 s, out 0.06 s), and a hum fades in.
End state: none. Position wraps modulo 10.85, so after the room you are back on the hero. Each scene comes to rest on its auto-centre pose (0.66; 2.35, 3.35, 4.35; 9.29).

Canvas or WebGL: yes. One WebGL2 canvas in a closed shadow root, created without antialias, depth, stencil or alpha; each scene renders into its own composer and render targets. DPR is min(device, 1.15) up to device DPR 2 and min(device, 1.5) above, with adaptive DPR (backing 1766 x 775 here). It draws everything: three scenes, the transition composite, the detail scene, and an orthographic UI scene holding all the text.
Code excerpt (rewritten from the minified bundle, not verbatim; values exact):
  const UNIT = 0.00075;                               // 1 unit = 1333 px of wheel delta
  const scenes = [{ h: 2.35 }, { h: 3 }, { h: 5.5 }]; // igloo, cubes, entry
  const TOTAL = 10.85;
  onWheel = e => { stopAutoCenter(); s.t2 += e.deltaY * UNIT; };
  onKey   = k => { s.t2 += (k === 'ArrowDown' ? 150 : k === 'ArrowUp' ? -150 : 0) * UNIT; };
  onDrag  = d => { s.t2 += d.delta01.y * 1.25; };     // full-height swipe = 1.25 units
  function render() {
    const prev = s.y;
    s.t1 = lerpFps(s.t1, s.t2, 0.075, 0.075);         // eased, max 0.075 units per frame
    s.y  = lerpFps(s.y, s.t1, 0.15);
    s.t2 = clamp(s.t2, s.y - 0.5625, s.y + 0.5625);
    s.vel = clamp((s.vel + Math.abs(s.y - prev)) * 0.98, 0, 1);
    const top = mod(s.y, TOTAL), bottom = mod(top + 1, TOTAL); // screen = 1 unit tall
    let t0 = 0, A = null, B = null, mix = 0;
    for (const sc of scenes) {
      sc.top = t0; t0 += sc.h;
      if (top >= sc.top && top < sc.top + sc.h) { A = sc; sc.p = (top + 1 - sc.top) / (sc.h + 1); }
      else if (bottom > sc.top && bottom <= sc.top + sc.h) {
        B = sc; mix = bottom - sc.top; sc.p = (bottom - sc.top) / (sc.h + 1); }
    }
    A.timeline.progress(A.p); A.renderToTexture();    // each scene scrubs its own timeline
    if (B) { B.timeline.progress(B.p); B.renderToTexture(); }
    composite.uProgress = mix;                         // ice-crack wipe across the overlap
    composite.uProgressVel = s.vel;
    if (idle > 1.4) gsap.to(s, { y: restPose(), duration: 2, ease: 'inOut3' });
  }

9. What makes it work, and what would be lost without WebGL or a cursor:
It feels expensive because nothing is a page. One heavily damped virtual scroll value scrubs keyframed cameras, so every notch is a dolly move, and every scene change is a material event (the frame cracks like ice with an RGB fringe) instead of a cut or a fade. Assets are used cleverly: the igloo is one batched mesh of 70 blocks whose breathe, push and explode motions are maths on centroids, with the bake cross-faded between assembled and exploded versions so the shading stays right as blocks part; the cubes fake refraction by rendering themselves twice; the particle sculptures are just 3D textures, so changing a logo is swapping a texture. Auto-centring after 1.4 s makes it feel authored even when the user stops halfway. The costs: 17 MB, a long blank preloader, a 5 s intro before scroll, no accessible text, no fallback. Without WebGL nothing remains (the only fallback is a one-line browser warning) because even the text is WebGL. A non-WebGL version needs pre-rendered sequences scrubbed by scroll plus DOM text, and loses the live hover reactions, refraction and per-pixel wipe. Without a cursor, scrolling still works (drag, swipe, taps), but block pushing with its labels, cube frost, particle stirring and camera parallax are gone.
