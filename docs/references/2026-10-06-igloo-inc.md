## What makes it work

1. One damped scroll value drives everything. Input adds to a target; a first follower chases it at 7.5% per frame (capped at 0.075 units, about 100 px, per frame), a second follower chases the first at 15%, and the target may never lead by more than 0.5625 units. A 100 px wheel notch therefore settles in about 0.5 s with no overshoot. The "expensive" feel is this number pair, not the 3D. GSAP ScrollTrigger's scrub-as-seconds is the same follower on native scroll.
2. Each scene is a paused GSAP timeline whose progress is set 1:1 from the scroll window (progress = (position + 1 - sceneTop) / (sceneHeight + 1)). Camera moves are keyframed in timeline time and scrubbed by scroll, so every notch is a dolly, forwards and backwards, and a scene can be authored and tuned as an ordinary timeline. This is the transferable grammar: pin a stage, build a timeline, seek it by scroll.
3. Authored rest poses. 1.4 s after the last input the position tweens to the nearest rest pose (0.66, 2.35, 3.35, 4.35, 9.29) over 1.6 to 2.4 s with an in-out ease. The user can stop anywhere, but the page always ends on a composed frame. Combined with the damping, this is what makes it feel directed rather than scrolled.
4. Assets are static bakes; motion is maths on the asset's parts. The igloo is 70 separate blocks, each pivoted at its own centroid with a random attribute, so breathe, hover-push and explode are one displacement vector per block scaled by a sine or a falloff. Two baked lightmaps (assembled, exploded) are cross-faded as blocks separate (mix = 5 x displacement) so shading stays right without live light. One asset set, many motions.
5. Scene changes are material events, not cuts. Across a 1-unit overlap both scenes render; a full-screen composite mixes them along a diagonal that a 1024 px RGB data texture breaks into ice-crack shapes (R silhouette, G blocky displacement, B slope wobble), scene A slides up by 0.4 x progress squared while B rises, and chromatic aberration peaks along the cut. The 1.29 MB data texture is the effect; the shader is 40 lines. Lesson: a mask asset plus a scrubbed threshold is a transition.
6. Scroll velocity is an input to the visuals: a friction-damped velocity (x0.98 per frame) drives the cubes' FOV kick (45 deg falling to 40 deg) and shader intensity, so fast scrolling looks fast. Cheap to copy with ScrollTrigger.getVelocity().
7. A screen-sized window that straddles scene boundaries, so the leaving scene is still being rendered while the arriving one enters. Nothing pops in at opacity 0; both are live during the hand-over.
8. Portrait is not a fallback. Cameras zoom out (zoom = aspect x 1.25) so the same scenes fit 390 px; swipe maps to 1.25 units per screen height with the same damping; nothing is dropped. The desktop-only parts are the pointer reactions (block push, frost, fluid stir, parallax), and they are additive, never load-bearing.
9. What it costs and what Skreed must not copy: 17.4 MB behind a 10 s preloader, an 8 s intro before scroll unlocks, no native scroll, no DOM text (every word is MSDF in WebGL, so no accessibility, no fallback, no SEO), and a mono typeface with glyph-scramble HUDs that read as tech, not product.

Where this site's own analysis differed from the reconciled rows above:

- The scroll grammar was proposed for phone too (0.7 s lag on the manifesto fill and the drift). Reconciled to laptop only: the drift is CSS scroll-driven on phones and cannot lag, and a lag on momentum scrolling reads as late, not expensive. The snap went behind a flag with a 0.4 s minimum delay, and the hero was excluded.
- The hero pipeline was proposed as a canvas-first hero with the 'luminosity' blend. Reconciled to an <img> with hard-light: luminosity would have made the 24 Frosty Whites and 24 Stormy Greys the same mid grey, and a canvas paints nothing without JS and is not an LCP candidate. The turntable moved from "two days, iPhone only" to phase 1, three hero devices, outside the launch plan.
- The Wall assembly was proposed as reversible with a 1 px neighbour ripple. Reconciled to forward only (scrolling back up finds the Wall whole) with the plain 4 px hover; the ripple is invisible at 44 px tiles and, made visible, a rule 48 tell. Displacement was cut from 120 to 320 px to 80 to 160 px and capped inside the section box.
- The charcoal cover edge and the load tiers were proposed as separate takes. See the table below for where they went.

## Asset lessons

- Separate the base from the light. Igloo ships two bakes (assembled, exploded) and mixes them by a number; Skreed ships one greyscale base per device, normalised to 50% grey, and one highlight mask, and mixes shade and finish at runtime. One base serves 240 shades x 2 finishes; a change of shade is a CSS custom property, a change of finish is an opacity.
- Author assets as separable parts with a known pivot, then animate the part's vector in code. Igloo's 70 blocks each carry a centroid attribute; Skreed's 240 tiles each carry a displacement vector from the grid centre. The asset does not move; the code moves it.
- The effect asset is a mask or a data layer, not a picture. Igloo's 1.29 MB RGB data texture is the wipe; its font is a 110 KB MSDF atlas. Skreed's equivalents are the base's alpha silhouette and the highlight mask: single channel, small, reused by the hero, the enlarged card, the quiz result, the reserve render and the share card.
- Motion that looks like video is scrubbed stills. Smoothness and weight are both set by frame count, so budget frames deliberately and derive every size and format from one master set with a repeatable script in the repo, never by hand. The weights in the spec are estimates until a real encode replaces them.
- Make the rest pose the poster. Igloo's hero rests at 0.66 of its timeline; the first thing Skreed paints is the A1 pose, which is frame 0 of any turntable, so the LCP image and the sequence's rest pose are the same file.
- Do the opposite of igloo on load: no wall of 110 files. The poster in HTML with fetchpriority high, every fourth frame on idle, the rest after, photographs lazy by distance; and every scrubbed stage must work with its first frame alone, because on Jio 4G it will have to for a second or two.
- Choose formats for the decoder you have. KTX2 and Basis are for GPUs; a 2D canvas wants AVIF with WebP fallback, greyscale with alpha, decoded off the main thread with createImageBitmap in a worker, backing store capped at DPR 2.
- Degrade by measurement, not by device name. Igloo steps DPR down 10% at a time while fps stays under 30; Skreed never loads frames on saveData, effectiveType 3g or deviceMemory <= 2, uses a timing gate on Safari, drops the laptop spiral and the Wall assembly on a weak GPU, and logs when a stage is turned before its frames arrive.
- Render once, reuse everywhere. Igloo reuses one mountain mesh five times; Skreed's one base per device is drawn by the hero, the Wall's enlarged card, the quiz result, the reserve column and the share card from one download.
- Leave the cursor out of the asset plan. Everything igloo does with the pointer is additive code over the same assets; nothing has to be rendered for it. Skreed's laptop extras are likewise code over the phone's assets, so there is one asset list, not two.
