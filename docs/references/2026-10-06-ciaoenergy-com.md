## What makes it work

1. Scroll is the playhead, not a trigger. One paused GSAP timeline holds one tween per section (duration = section height / total height) and every frame calls tl.seek(scroll / total). The 3D state is a pure function of scroll position; nothing is timed, nothing waits for an observer, and letting go anywhere leaves a coherent frame. This is the mechanism under everything Sam liked.
2. One object, hand-set poses, one per beat. Every section top is a row in a keyframe table (camera position, FOV, can rotation, spacing, light intensities, spotlight height). The page is eleven 125svh spacers under fixed overlays, so the composition at each beat is designed by a person, not emergent from physics. Paging (and the desktop settle-to-nearest) guarantees the viewer lands on a composed frame.
3. The product is the content. The four benefits are printed on the can's 2048 px label texture; the benefits beat adds no graphics. It turns the can round, drops the fill light and slides the printed list through a fixed light band while the DOM repeats each line in big type. The asset does the storytelling, the code only reads it.
4. Light is the narrator. Key lights go to 0, the environment tint drops to 0.2, and a 2.7 KB crescent gobo switches on. The can rises 0.3 units per beat so the next printed line enters the fixed light; the object moves through the light, not the light over the object. A tiny mask asset carries the whole middle of the page.
5. Colour is a page-wide variable. Two registered @property colours per flavour, with a 0.6 s transition on :root, repaint the radial wash, the SVG tagline fill and the loop-video choice. Picking a flavour repaints the site and the cross-fade comes free from CSS.
6. One asset, many instances. A 163 KB can is cloned 24 times (12 on mobile); six label textures give six flavours; one HDR is multiplied by a per-section tint. Variety comes from data and from reuse, not from files. At 5 MB the site is heavy, but the heavy parts (HDR, textures) are shared by every beat.
7. Fixed overlays over empty spacers. Text lives in fixed full-viewport containers toggled per section with autoAlpha; the canvas is fixed; layout never moves. Only the scene and opacity change, so there is no layout thrash and the DOM layer survives without WebGL.
8. The carousel feel is three numbers. Slot x wraps around the ring; centredness p = 1 - |x| / 3.5 is 1 only for the centre can; position eases exponentially at 10/s (89% in 200 ms); release flings by 8x and snaps. Scale, tilt, label spin and pointer influence are all multiplied by p, so only the centre item responds and the rest recede by rule.
9. One text grammar reused everywhere. SplitText masks, yPercent 110 to 0, power3.out, 0.6 to 0.7 s, 0.08 s per line; out to -110. A strike line is a registered length variable scaled 0 to 1. Everything on the DOM side is the same move at different sizes.
10. Atmosphere is pre-rendered and lazy. Six 5 s smoke loops (0.9 to 1.1 MB each) have preload none and 12 KB AVIF posters, and only the active flavour's loop loads, only while section 7 is in view.
11. A low-power path is a designed branch, not a degraded one: under 1024 px, 12 cans, standard material, no bloom, no SMAA, DPR capped at 2, keyframes unchanged. The phone gets the same beats, cropped.
12. Weak spots to not copy: a 7 s loader video plus a 4.5 s intro before scroll unlocks, a wheel listener that cancels native scroll and locks for 1 to 1.5 s per notch, a pointer tilt that is not centred (7 degrees at screen middle), and a page that loops with no end.

Where this site's own analysis differed from the reconciled rows above:

- The hero turn was proposed as a 250svh sticky stage with three scroll beats on both phone and laptop, a 1.6x laptop close-up, and a 'multiply' tint. Reconciled: the hero stays one screen, the turn is driven by the gyroscope and the pointer rather than the scroll, there is no close-up, and the tint is hard-light. Multiply can only darken, so the 50 shades under 0.1 luminance (Charcoal included, the hero's starting shade and the October 31 shade) would collapse to silhouettes. The 250svh stage pushed the Wall 1.5 screens down on the primary device and left a full screen with no CTA (G2, G11).
- The settle-to-nearest-beat on the hero stage (snapTo [0, 0.33, 0.66, 1], delay 0.1 s) was dropped. A scrubbed sequence is readable at every position so the tween solves nothing, 0.1 s fights trackpad inertia, and it is a second scroll owner beside Lenis. The only settle that survives is inside the scrubStage utility, on the two pinned stages where a mid position is genuinely uncomposed, behind Sam's flag with a 0.4 s minimum delay.
- The registered shade pair was proposed with the count line as a carrier and --shade-today set from JavaScript. Reconciled: the count line is not a carrier; --shade-today is written into the <html> style attribute at build time, never from JavaScript, and only --shade carries the transition, so nothing fades on first paint.
- The name swap was estimated at 0.25 day with SplitText "already loaded for the manifesto". Reconciled to 0.5 day with SplitText loaded inside the Wall island, not the critical bundle, plus the aria-label while split.
- The vendor brief asked for a "neutral light grey as if the shade were Pearl" material and no specular pass. Reconciled: diffuse base at neutral 50% grey (so hard-light passes the shade through) and a second specular-on-black pass exported as alpha, because the two-pass asset is what makes the finish a layer rather than a second render.
- The fallbacks named here (a two-angle crossfade of A1 plus side profile, a Morflax export with the Tough mesh) were struck: neither asset exists.

## Asset lessons

- Render the product neutral and let data colour it. Ciao uses one 163 KB can and six label textures; Skreed uses one greyscale base per hero device and the hex in shades-240.json. One base serves 240 shades at the weight of one, and a shade is a 7-byte string, not a file. Never request a render per shade for the hero or the turn; the 5,040 Dropbox renders are for the reserve picker, the OG images and calibration.
- Frame 0 of any sequence is the hero pose at the hero's resolution, so the LCP image and the sequence's first frame are the same render. The stage is never blank, no skeleton is needed beyond a sized box, and the turn starts from exactly what the visitor is already looking at. This is a hard requirement in the vendor brief.
- The moving light is a mask, not a render. Ciao's whole benefits beat runs on a 2.7 KB gobo. Skreed's gloss highlight is one soft mask under 5 KB plus, if the turntable ships, a per-frame offset table, blended with screen and driven by the gyroscope on phone and the pointer on laptop through one function. The turntable's specular pass exists so the highlight can be exact per frame; the 21 single-angle device heroes get the mask only.
- Only print copy on the product where it is literally on the product. Ciao's benefits work because they are on the label. Skreed has nothing printed except the mark, so the readable content is the surface itself (matte, gloss) and the shade. Do not fake a label, a sticker or a caption on the render.
- Size textures to the closest read, not the default view, and replace every size estimate with a real encode. The spec's 20 KB a phone frame and 40 KB a laptop frame are placeholders until a sharp encode of three vendor frames on day 3 prints the real numbers.
- Keyframes are data. Ciao's choreography is one KEYFRAMES array of hand-set numbers. Skreed's rest poses are labels declared in each stage's config, and if the turn ships its offsets live in offsets.json, so the review skill grades numbers, not adjectives, and a change to the choreography is a data edit.
- Atmosphere loads only in its beat and announces itself with a tiny poster. Ciao's 1 MB loops have preload none and 12 KB posters. Skreed's lifestyle photographs get LQIP AVIF posters and lazy loading per card; nothing beyond the hero image is in the first view.
- A low-power path is designed, not degraded. Ciao halves the object count under 1024 px with the keyframes unchanged. Skreed's phone gets 16 frames at 640 px and never loads frames on saveData, 3g or low memory, with the finish toggle doing the work instead.
- Registered custom properties with a root transition are the cheapest cross-fade on the web. Use them for the two shade variables and let CSS do the tween; write JavaScript only to set the value.
- Weigh the reference before copying its generosity. Ciao is 5 MB and 11.5 s before scroll; Skreed is under 1.5 MB first view and 0 s. Everything Ciao front-loads (HDR, textures, video), Skreed defers or replaces with data. The feeling of one object moving between composed poses costs nothing; the assets are what cost.
- Write the render request in the vendor's language: pose, start angle, step angle, frame count, material, passes, background, size, naming. The brief is in the README's asset additions. If the vendor cannot confirm the scene by build day 2, there is no turntable and the hero keeps the finish toggle.
